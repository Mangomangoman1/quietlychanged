Sling Blue Price Increase Only Affects 15 TV Markets
February 11, 2026
Written By:
Jim Kimble
Sling TV is raising prices in 2026. But the change is limited to a small slice of the country.
Only Sling Blue subscribers who get local ABC, NBC, or FOX stations are affected. If you don’t get locals through Sling, your monthly price stays at $46.
New customers are already paying the new rate, but existing customers can expect to see an increase to their monthly bill on Feb. 20.
Which Sling TV plans cost more now?
The price hike impacts subscribers of Sling Blue in 15 TV markets across the U.S. There are more than 210 designated TV markets nationwide.
Only five markets get all three local stations through Sling: Chicago, Los Angeles, New York, Philadelphia, and San Francisco.
Three markets get two local channels. Seven markets get just one.
If you’re unsure whether Sling offers locals in your area, Antenna Land maintains a chart of local channels on Sling TV
.
Sling says on a support page that, “The cost we pay to local broadcast networks in order to provide you with the programming you count on continues to rise significantly — well above the price of inflation. Local channels are the most expensive part of any channel lineup, with costs passed down from the local broadcast networks.”
Is Sling Select getting a price increase?
Sling Select, a smaller version of the Blue plan, also includes local channels in these same 15 markets. But the Select plan is not impacted by the new price hike.
The Select plan has about 10 channels, including FOX News and MeTV, starting at $19.99. Subscribers already pay $5 more for one to two locals, and $10 more for ABC, NBC and FOX.
Sling Select subscribers will get local channels if they live in a TV market where locals are available on the Blue plan.
How to Add Local Channels for Free with Sling TV
In most of the U.S., Sling doesn’t carry local stations anyway. That has been its quiet strength. By skipping local broadcast fees in most markets, Sling has kept its base price lower than competitors.
Over the years, Sling has encouraged its customers to use a TV antenna for locals. For a time, the company even gave antennas to customers who prepaid for a subscription.
Adding a TV antenna
to your Smart TV is the easiest way to expand your channel lineup for free. You can also add your own DVR for antenna channels if you want to record broadcast channels.
Why Sling is Changing Its Plans and Prices
For years, Sling TV was the clear budget option, especially for people who wanted channels like ESPN or Big Ten Network.
But with the skinny bundle era now underway, Sling is no longer the only low-cost option. Within the last year, Sling launched its Select plan and a Day Pass program that gives customers access to its channel lineup for hours instead of a full month.
Disney and FOX, which supply many of Sling’s sports channels, now offer their own skinny bundles: ESPN Unlimited and FOX One.
DIRECTV has its own line of smaller, genre-based channel packages.
Budget-friendly services like Frndly and Philo avoid local broadcast channels altogether, focusing instead on entertainment cable networks.
And YouTube TV announced the first round of its skinny packages this week, breaking apart its larger bundle into plans geared for sports fans and entertainment-focused customers.
The result is more volatility in pricing and packaging across the industry. Local channels remain one of the most expensive pieces of any bundle.
They’re also the one part viewers can still get free with an antenna.
