## Reddit r/ouraring (text post; read the sub rules first. If self-promo links aren't allowed, link only the two Oura pages)
Title: PSA: what's actually in the new Terms of Use you have to accept by Nov 4
Body:
Oura's help article says you have to accept the updated terms in the app by November 4, 2026, and "After that date, you can't use the Oura App until you accept." I read the new terms (Last Updated Oct 2) against the April version. The main changes for U.S. members:
- Arbitration section went from one paragraph to 12 parts. It now spells out a class action and jury trial waiver and covers any dispute "regardless of when it accrued."
- Before you can file anything you have to mail a written Pre-Dispute Notice and wait 30 days.
- New mass-arbitration rules: if 25+ similar claims are filed, 20 random ones go first and the rest are paused.
- There's no arbitration opt-out (there wasn't one before either). If you don't like the new terms, "your only recourse is to cease using the Services."
- The promise to email you at least 30 days before a fee change is replaced with notice "within the timeframe required by applicable law," plus a right to cancel before the new fee.
- Liability cap changes from $100 to what you paid in the last 12 months.
Oura says the update doesn't change how it handles your data, and the Privacy Policy is unchanged (June 15).
For comparison, WHOOP and Eight Sleep both offer a 30-day arbitration opt-out.
Sources: https://ouraring.com/terms-and-conditions and https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/55998083760147-Updates-to-the-Oura-Terms-of-Use

Subject: Oura will lock its app Nov 4 unless members accept new arbitration terms
Hi Victoria,
Oura's new Terms of Use (dated Oct 2) take effect Nov 4, and its help center says members have to accept them in the app by then: "After that date, you can't use the Oura App until you accept." Oura describes the update as clarifying dispute resolution, but the U.S. arbitration section goes from one paragraph to 12 parts. It adds an explicit jury trial waiver, a mandatory 30-day pre-dispute notice by mail, and mass-arbitration rules that send claims through 20 at a time. There's still no opt-out, and the terms say the "only recourse" for anyone who disagrees is to stop using the service.
That's out of step with Oura's closest rivals. WHOOP's terms include a 30-day arbitration opt-out and let members reject updated terms by email, and Eight Sleep also offers a 30-day opt-out. Oura also replaced its promise of 30 days' email notice before fee changes with "within the timeframe required by applicable law."
I haven't found any news coverage yet. The write-up quotes the old and new language: https://quietlychanged.com/changes/2026-10-17-oura-app-locks-nov-4-unless-you-accept-new-arbitration-terms/
