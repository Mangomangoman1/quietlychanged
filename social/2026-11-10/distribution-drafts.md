Hi Michael,

In case it's useful: Dropbox's rewritten Terms of Service, posted Sept 1 and effective January 1, 2027, make three changes that hit ordinary users:

1. Free accounts can be suspended or closed after "6 consecutive months" without access. Today the limit is 12. Dropbox's inactive-account help page still describes the 12-month rule.
2. Minimum age rises to 18 (from 13 in the US, 16 elsewhere): "Dropbox may use and rely on information from third parties, including age signals from app stores, for the purpose of enforcing this restriction." The pages don't say what happens to existing teen accounts.

For context on #1, from each company's current terms or help pages (checked Nov 10): Google allows 2 years, Microsoft gives Outlook.com and OneDrive 1 year, iCloud 1 year, pCloud 12 months, and MEGA over 3 months.

- pCloud: free accounts "inactive for 12 months, will be permanently deleted"
- Dropbox: 12 months today, 6 from Jan 1. Its help page counts log-ins and adding, editing or deleting files, but not activity on Dropbox Sign or DocSend
- MEGA: free accounts inactive "for over 3 months", after notice

The Dropbox rewrite also sets an 18+ minimum (so teen accounts are affected) and says simply keeping an account means you accept the new terms.

Title: PSA: from Jan 1, 2027, free Dropbox accounts can be closed after 6 months of inactivity (currently 12)

Body:
Dropbox's updated Terms of Service take effect January 1, 2027. A few changes worth knowing if you have a free (Basic) account you rarely open:

- Inactivity: the new terms let Dropbox suspend or terminate access if you "don't have a Paid Account and haven't accessed our Services for 6 consecutive months". The current terms say 12. Dropbox says it will email notice first.
- The inactive-account help page still describes the 12-month rule ("Logging in again will reset the 12-month clock"). It counts log-ins and adding, editing or deleting files. Activity on Dropbox Sign or DocSend doesn't count.
- Age: "you must be at least 18" (currently 13 in the US, 16 elsewhere), and Dropbox may use "age signals from app stores" to enforce it.
- Acceptance: "By maintaining an account after the updates come into effect, you agree to be bound by the revised Terms." If you don't agree, the terms say to "cancel and delete your account" before Jan 1.

If you have an old free account with files you care about, sign in before Jan 1 and check the email address on it for inactivity notices.
