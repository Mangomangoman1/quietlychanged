Hi,

A tip that may fit your digital ID coverage. Xero, the small-business accounting platform, updated its Terms of Use and privacy notice on October 26, 2026. Nothing has been reported on it yet as far as we can find.

- New section 12 of the terms: "we may ask you to provide things like government-issued identification documents and a biometric facial scan so we can confirm the document belongs to you." If a customer can't be verified or doesn't respond in a reasonable time, Xero "may decline to activate your subscription or restrict or terminate access to some or all of our services."
- New section 29 adds ongoing automated risk screening that can suspend access or payment features while Xero looks into a flag.
- The privacy notice now says Xero "may collect facial imagery and biometric data (including facial recognition templates) to match you to your identity documents and ensure it really is you when accessing our services through your login." So face matching isn't limited to onboarding. Verification partners "may act as independent data controllers for certain activities" and may use anonymised or aggregated data to improve fraud models.
- The changes apply now for new customers and from December 28, 2026 for existing ones. The previous privacy notice (February 2025) didn't mention biometrics.

Hi Chris,

A heads-up that may matter to firms with clients on Xero. Xero posted new Terms of Use, new US arbitration terms and a new privacy notice on October 26, 2026. They apply to existing customers from December 28.

- Identity checks (new s.12): Xero can ask for government ID and "a biometric facial scan," and can restrict or terminate access if a customer isn't verified in a reasonable time. A new s.29 adds ongoing automated screening that can suspend access or payment features, and can trigger fresh checks after a change in ownership or business structure. For a client whose invoicing, bills and payroll live in Xero, that's a real access risk.
- US disputes: one generic arbitration sentence became a ten-part US contract. It requires a Notice of Claim to legalnotices@xero.com and a personal video or phone meeting within 60 days, and it says "You'll need to bring any claim within one year of when you knew, or reasonably should have known, of the issue." Proceedings are confidential, and 25 or more coordinated claims get batched. We found no opt-out.
- Bank feeds (s.31): connected aggregators "disclaim all liability for any loss, theft, compromise, or misuse whatsoever in connection with their service (including negligence)."
