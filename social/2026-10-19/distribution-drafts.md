Title: PSA: New USPS rule (in effect Oct 19) lets USPS invoice you for a buyer's unpaid foreign duties, even if the package is lost or returned
Body:
If you ship sales overseas yourself with USPS (not through eBay International Shipping, where you ship to a US hub and eBay collects import fees from the buyer), this one matters.

USPS published an interim final rule in the Federal Register on Oct 19 that took effect the same day (FR Doc. 2026-21310). It adds a new section 424 to the International Mail Manual:

- If a customs authority, postal operator or broker in the destination country tries to collect unpaid duties, taxes or import fees, "the Postal Service (or its representative) will send an invoice to the mailer for such amounts."
- You have to pay "the entire amount due in accordance with the invoice terms, even if the mailer disputes the assessment," and you're still on the hook "regardless of whether the item is delivered, lost, damaged, or returned."
- Late payment accrues 6% a year in interest, plus USPS's collection costs.
- The only thing you can dispute with USPS is an accounting error on its invoice. Arguments about the foreign charge itself go directly to that country's customs or post.
- Where the destination requires delivered duty paid, you must ship DDP.

USPS says the trigger is the EU ending duty-free entry for low-value goods on July 1. EU countries now charge a flat €3 duty per item type (one T-shirt plus one watch counts as two items, so €6) plus VAT and fees. Product identifiers become mandatory in EU customs data on Nov 1.

What you can do:
- Use USPS Delivered Duty Paid when it's offered. It's only available at the time of mailing (counter, Click-N-Ship, USPS APIs, GSS), only on Priority Mail Express Intl, Priority Mail Intl and First-Class Package Intl, and only to select countries. USPS's July 1 Postal Bulletin added 21 destinations, including France, Italy, Spain and the Netherlands.
- Describe items accurately on the customs form. USPS assigns HS codes from your descriptions and charges the €3 "for each unique HS Code."
- For context, FedEx, UPS and DHL already bill the shipper when the recipient won't pay. USPS is catching up, with stricter wording.

Comments are open until Nov 18. Per the notice, email PCFederalRegister@usps.gov with the subject "Customs Requirements and Payment Procedures" and include your name and address. The document isn't open for comment on regulations.gov.

Sources:
- Rule: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/19/2026-21310/customs-requirements-and-payment-procedures-for-certain-customs-charges
- USPS prepaid duties page: https://www.usps.com/international/prepaid-import-duties.htm
- USPS Postal Bulletin, DDP countries: https://about.usps.com/postal-bulletin/2026/pb22707/html/updt_002.htm

You covered the launch of USPS Delivered Duty Paid in February. Today's Federal Register has the enforcement side. It's an interim final rule, effective today, and it doesn't seem to have been reported beyond a couple of small blogs.

FR Doc. 2026-21310 ("Customs Requirements and Payment Procedures for Certain Customs Charges") adds a new IMM section 424:
