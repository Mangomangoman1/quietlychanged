Subscription Black Friday deals 2026: what's actually on sale, the price after, and when each deal ends

Short answer: as of November 23, 2026 the biggest subscription Black Friday deals on official pages are HBO Max Basic With Ads at $5.49 a month for 12 months (then $10.99), Peacock Select at $29.99 for a year or $2.99 a month for 3 months, Paramount+ at $2.99 a month for 2 months, Apple TV at $6.99 a month for 6 months (then $14.99) and PlayStation Plus 12-month plans at 20% to 33% off. Most end November 30. Almost all are for new or lapsed subscribers only, and every one renews at full price unless you cancel. Disney+ added the Disney+, Hulu Bundle at $4.99 a month for 12 months on November 24, and Dropbox added 3 free months on yearly plans until December 1 on November 25. STARZ was offering a year for $11.99 (then $69.99 a year) when we checked on November 27. Amazon Prime and Netflix showed no Black Friday discount on their own sites when we checked.

Published November 23, 2026 · Updated November 27, 2026

Every price below was on the company's own website when we checked on November 23, 2026 (US prices, before tax), unless we say otherwise. We left out deals we could only find on third-party sites. "Our math" marks totals and savings we calculated ourselves. Deals can change or end early, so check the linked page before you pay; we'll update this page as offers change through Cyber Monday.

When does the price go back up?

This is the part the deal headlines leave out. Every offer here is an auto-renewing subscription: when the promo period ends, you are charged the regular price unless you cancel first. If you sign up on Black Friday, November 27, the full price kicks in around these dates (our math; the exact day depends on your billing date):

  • Late January 2027: Paramount+ (after 2 months) and the YouTube TV main-plan offer (after 2 months).
  • Late February 2027: Peacock's 3-month offers, Audible's $0.99 months and Spotify's 3 free months.
  • Late May 2027: Apple TV (after 6 months) and the YouTube TV Sports + News offer (after 6 months).
  • Late November 2027: HBO Max and the Disney+, Hulu Bundle (after 12 months), Peacock's 1-year offers, PlayStation Plus 12-month plans and Microsoft 365's discounted first year.
  • Late February 2028: Dropbox's yearly plans bought with 3 free months (after 15 months).

Put a reminder in your calendar a few days before each date. Several services say up front that you won't get money back once you've been charged: HBO Max says "Subscription fees are non-refundable.", Peacock says "no credits or refunds will be provided for subscription fees already paid." and YouTube TV's offers say "No refunds. Cancel anytime."

Streaming deals

HBO Max: $5.49 a month for 12 months

HBO Max's offer is the longest monthly discount on this list. The terms: "Offer valid from November 23, 2026, through November 30, 2026." It is for new and returning customers on hbomax.com and through Apple, Google Play, Roku, Samsung and Xumo, but only new customers through Amazon Fire. You get "the HBO Max Basic With Ads monthly plan at a discounted rate of $5.49 per month (plus applicable taxes) for the first 12 months." After that it renews at "the then-current subscription price (now, $10.99/mo. plus applicable taxes)".

Two catches: "If you switch plans during your discount period, you will lose the benefit of this offer." So upgrading to ad-free Standard or Premium ends the discount. And it only covers Basic With Ads, which streams on 2 devices in 1080p. Plans and regular prices: our HBO Max price guide.

Peacock: up to 66% off, monthly or annual

Peacock's Black Friday code is valid "November 23, 2026 – November 30, 2026" and gives four choices: Select for "3 months at $2.99/month" or "1 year for $29.99", and Premium for "3 months at $8.99/month" or "1 year for $89.99". The "up to 66% off" headline is the Select annual deal: $29.99 instead of $89.99. Our math: the annual deals beat the monthly ones by far. Select annual costs $29.99 for 12 months; Select monthly costs $8.97 for 3 months and then $8.99 a month.

Who can't get it: you must not be "a current paying Peacock subscriber", and the offer "Cannot be combined with any other offer, including any offer redeemed in the past 12 months." Select doesn't include sports, movies or live events: Peacock says it "does not include Sports, Movies, Live Programming (including the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade) or Peacock Original series." If you want live sports or the parade, take the Premium deal. More: our Peacock price guide.

Paramount+: $2.99 a month for 2 months

Paramount+ calls it a "Limited-time Black Friday offer! Get any monthly plan for $2.99/month for your first 2 months." The small print: "Ends 11/30/26. New & eligible former subscribers. Monthly plans only." The deal applies to both Essential ($8.99 a month, with ads) and Premium ($13.99), so Premium is the better pick: you save $22.00 over two months instead of $12.00 (our math). After two months it "auto-renews at then-current full price of your plan unless canceled." Annual plans aren't included. More: our Paramount+ price guide.

Apple TV: $6.99 a month for 6 months

Apple's page says: "Get 6 months of Apple TV for just $6.99 per month and save over 50%. Offer ends November 30." The fine print is precise: "Offer expires on November 30, 2026, at 8:00 p.m. PT." It is for "eligible new and qualified returning subscribers", and only if you're billed by Apple: it is "not available if (i) you are eligible for a three months free Apple TV offer or (ii) your subscription to Apple TV is billed through a third party or your mobile wireless provider." Then it "automatically renews at $14.99/mo." We logged the deal the day it appeared: Apple TV's Black Friday deal. More: our Apple TV price guide.

STARZ: $12 for a year (added Nov 27)

When we checked on November 27, STARZ's home page was headlined "BEST DEAL OF THE YEAR" with "Only $12 for 1 year"; the offer itself is $11.99 for 12 months. The fine print: "Limited time offer. Offer available to new STARZ App subscribers who subscribe via starz.com. Offer does not include free trial. After completion of 12 month offer, service automatically renews for additional 12 month terms at the then current price (currently $69.99 per 12 month term plus applicable taxes) unless cancelled. Subscription fee is non-refundable." STARZ doesn't give an end date. Our math: $58.00 less than the $69.99 yearly price for the first year. The catch is the renewal: it rolls into another full year at $69.99, not a monthly plan, so if you only want the cheap year, turn off auto-renew before the 12 months are up. Because the fee is non-refundable, cancelling partway through won't get money back.

YouTube TV: new-user offers until December 2

YouTube TV's deals run two days past the others. From its welcome page: "New YouTube TV users pay $59.99/mo for 2 months of the main YouTube TV plan (save $46), then $82.99/mo thereafter. Ends 12/2/2026." and "New YouTube TV users pay $49.99/mo for 6 months of Sports + News Plan (save $132), then $71.99/mo thereafter." Existing members get nothing. The free trial is shorter now: since November 26 every plan shows a "2-day free trial" (it was 10 days on most plans on November 20), so a trial won't carry you far past Black Friday weekend. Switching plans during the promo loses it; the rules, add-on prices and NFL Sunday Ticket are in our YouTube TV price guide.

Disney+, Hulu Bundle: $4.99 a month for 12 months (added Nov 24)

Disney added a Black Friday offer on November 24: the Disney+, Hulu Bundle with ads "is $12.99/mo $4.99/mo for a year this Black Friday. Offer ends November 30", and the no-ads Premium bundle is $14.99 a month for a year instead of $21.99. The terms: "$4.99/mo. for 12 mos., then auto-renews at $12.99/mo. or then-current regular monthly price" (Premium "then auto-renews at $21.99/mo."), "Ends 11:59 PM PST on 11/30/26. U.S. only, 18+." Current customers are out: "Disney+, ESPN, Hulu, and bundle subscribers are not eligible." And like HBO Max, "Any plan switch after redemption of this offer will result in forfeiture of the discount pricing." The 6-month $11.99 option we saw on November 23 is no longer shown. Our write-up of the change.

No Black Friday discount (yet): Amazon Prime, Netflix

  • Hulu on its own: we found Disney's offer on disneyplus.com (above); we haven't re-checked hulu.com/welcome or hulu.com/start since November 23, when neither showed a Black Friday price. Details in our Disney+ guide and Hulu guide.
  • Amazon Prime: Amazon's Black Friday Week "kicked off at 12:01 a.m. PST on November 19 and will run through November 30", but Prime itself isn't discounted. Amazon's own answer to "How do I join Prime before Black Friday?" is that anyone can join "for $14.99 a month or $139 a year, or start a" free 30-day trial if eligible. See our Amazon Prime guide for the trial timing.
  • Netflix: its plans help page says "We do not offer annual, multi-month, or lifetime membership plans." and lists no promotion. Prices: our Netflix guide.

Gaming, music, audiobooks and software

PlayStation Plus: 12-month plans 20% to 33% off

PlayStation's page says "Join PlayStation Plus Premium before November 30 and get a special Black Friday discount.*" and the asterisk means "Qualifying users only." The 12-month prices in the page data on November 23 (the same as on November 16): Essential $63.99 instead of $79.99 (20% off), Extra $101.24 instead of $134.99 (25%) and Premium $107.19 instead of $159.99 (33%). Our math: discounted Premium costs $27.80 less than a regular year of Extra. The page asks you to "Sign in to see offer", so your account may see a different price. Stacking, renewal price and history: our PS Plus guide.

Microsoft 365: 30% off the first month or year

Microsoft's compare page leads with "Save 30% and get more from Microsoft 365". Personal is $6.99 for the first month (regularly $9.99) or $69.99 for the first year ($99.99); Premium is $13.99 for the first month ($19.99) or $139.99 for the first year ($199.99). Family isn't discounted ($12.99 a month or $129.99 a year). The fine print: "Offer available while advertised. Discount on first billing period." and "Customers who have redeemed a Microsoft 365 trial offer are not eligible." The offer isn't new for Black Friday: we first logged it on November 11.

Spotify Premium: 3 months free until December 31

Spotify's US Premium page: "$0 for 3 months, then $12.99 per month after. Offer only available if you haven't tried Premium before." and "Offer ends December 31, 2026." It's Premium Individual only. This is the longest window on the list, so there's no need to rush on Black Friday. We logged the switch from a 1-month trial on November 19; prices for every plan are in our Spotify guide.

Audible: $0.99 a month for 3 months, or $99 for a year of Premium

Audible's plans page has two offers. The banner: "This Season's Biggest Deal | $0.99/mo for 3 months + $20 Audible bonus", then "$8.99/mo thereafter—terms apply." ($8.99 is Standard, which lets you pick 1 audiobook a month and listen while subscribed.) Further down: "LIMITED TIME OFFER | $99 for a year of Premium" ("That's like $8.25/mo."). Our math: that's $50.50 less than Audible's regular Premium Annual at $149.50 (12 audiobooks) and $80.40 less than 12 months of Premium at $14.95. Audible doesn't say how many credits the $99 year includes, or when either offer ends, so check the checkout page. We first logged the $99 offer on October 16.

cPanel: 50% off new licenses until December 7, then a price increase

For web hosts and freelancers: cPanel's pricing page says "Black Friday: 50% off new licenses. Till December 7." Solo shows $13.73 a month billed annually instead of $27.46, Admin $16.48 instead of $32.96, Pro $24.73 instead of $49.46, and Premier $34.99 a month instead of $69.99. The same page still carries this notice: "effective December 16, 2026, cPanel license pricing will be adjusted, affecting new orders and all renewals on or after that date." So the sale ends nine days before every renewal gets more expensive. Details: cPanel's 50% off sale and the Dec 16 increase.

Dropbox: 3 free months on yearly plans until December 1 (added Nov 25)

Dropbox's plans page added "Get 3 free months. Offer ends December 1." on November 25. It isn't a price cut: "The displayed monthly price for each plan is its annual purchase price divided by 15 months, which includes the 3 free months offered through this promotion." So Plus (2 TB) shows $7.99 a month instead of $9.99, Standard $12 per user instead of $15, and Advanced $19.20 per user instead of $24. Our math: Plus costs $119.88 for 15 months, $29.97 less than paying the yearly rate for those extra 3 months. The catch: "After the first 15 months, your subscription renews every 12 months at the annual renewal price shown at checkout until you cancel. Offer valid for new subscriptions only." Details: Dropbox's Black Friday deal.

Price increases arriving right after the sales

Black Friday banners sit next to price notices. Three we're tracking: Logos and Verbum subscriptions go up $1 to $2 a month on December 7 (Logos's site meanwhile advertises "Black Friday Starts Now: Up to 75% Off. More Deals Drop Nov. 27.", which doesn't change the subscription increase), Runna's UK prices rise from the first renewal on or after December 10, and cPanel on December 16. Every price change we've logged is on our subscription price history page.

How to get a Black Friday subscription deal without overpaying later

  1. Check eligibility first. Most of these are for new or lapsed subscribers. If you're paying now, you usually can't switch to the deal; Peacock also excludes anyone who used an offer in the past 12 months.
  2. Prefer the longest discount. HBO Max's 12 months and Peacock's annual codes lock the price for a year; 2- and 3-month deals end before spring.
  3. Pick the plan before you sign up. HBO Max and YouTube TV both drop the promo if you switch plans mid-offer.
  4. Sign up directly when it matters. Apple TV's deal is not available if you're billed through a carrier or third party; HBO Max's only covers new customers via Amazon Fire.
  5. Set a reminder before each renewal date (see the dates above). Refunds after a renewal charge are unlikely: HBO Max, Peacock and YouTube TV all say so.
  6. Watch for price notices. Companies can raise the regular price your deal renews into. We check these pages every morning and publish each change the same day.
