Amazon Prime price (November 2026): monthly vs yearly, Prime for Young Adults, Prime Access, Prime Video Ultra and every price increase

Short answer: when we checked Amazon's Prime fee page on November 19, 2026, Amazon Prime was $14.99 a month or $139 a year in the US, before tax. Prime for Young Adults was $7.49 a month or $69 a year, Prime Access $6.99 a month, and Prime Video alone $8.99 a month. Prime's own price last went up on February 18, 2022; the only increase since was the ad-free Prime Video add-on, now Prime Video Ultra at $4.99.

Published November 19, 2026

Amazon's help page lists "Current Amazon Prime membership pricing" as "$14.99 per month" and "$139 per year," and says "Prime Video membership is $8.99 per month." These match our sourced Amazon Prime price history: the main US prices have not changed since February 18, 2022. The help page also warns: "Your Prime Membership may be subject to sales tax in some states."

The one US price Amazon has raised since then is the ad-free add-on. On April 10, 2026, Prime Video's $2.99-a-month Ad Free option became Prime Video Ultra at $4.99 a month. Amazon's announcement says: "There is no change in cost to the Amazon Prime membership."

Black Friday 2026: there is no discount on Prime itself

Amazon's Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday sale started "at 12:01 a.m. PST on November 19" and runs "through November 30." Amazon's own page doesn't list a cheaper membership. Its answer to "How do I join Prime before Black Friday?" is: "Anyone can join Prime for $14.99 a month or $139 a year, or start a free 30-day trial, if eligible."

What the page does list for members: a $200 Amazon gift card on approval for Prime Visa ("November 5 to January 7, 2027") or $100 for Prime Store Card ("November 22–30"), and, "On November 24 only," 20% cash back on selected products for Prime for Young Adults members.

If you only want Prime for the sale, the 30-day free trial (if Amazon offers you one) covers November 19 to 30 with days to spare. Turn off renewal before it ends, or you are charged: Amazon's help page says "you'll be upgraded to a paid membership plan automatically at the end of the trial period." Once you have used Prime in a paid period, that period generally isn't refundable (see the refund rules below).

Monthly or yearly: which is cheaper?

Our math, at the November 19 prices:

  • Prime: 12 months at $14.99 = $179.88, against $139 for the year. The yearly plan saves $40.88 (about 23%). Nine months of the monthly plan cost $134.91 and ten cost $149.90, so the annual plan only wins if you'd keep Prime 10 months or more.
  • Prime for Young Adults: 12 × $7.49 = $89.88, against $69 a year: $20.88 saved, and again the yearly plan only pays off at 10 months or more.
  • Prime Access has no yearly price. At $6.99 a month it costs $83.88 a year, which is $55.12 less than yearly Prime.
  • No ads on Prime Video: Prime plus Prime Video Ultra is $14.99 + $4.99 = $19.98 a month, or $139 + $45.99 = $184.99 a year. Ultra's yearly price is $13.89 less than 12 monthly payments ($59.88); Amazon calls it "a 23% discount from the monthly rate."

Monthly members have more flexibility: Amazon's pause page lets a Prime Monthly member pause for one month with automatic resume, and either plan can pause "for up to one year."

Prime for Young Adults: half price, plus a 6-month trial

Prime for Young Adults is for "18-24 year-olds and students." The sign-up page currently offers a "6-month trial for $0" ("Trial courtesy of Uber Eats. After trial, auto-renews at $7.49/month.") and says members get "full Prime benefits and 5% cash back" on eligible items.

The catch is sharing. The same page says: "Prime for Young Adults does not include Household sharing of Prime benefits." Amazon's pause page also lists "Have a Student Prime membership" among the memberships that can't be paused.

Prime Access: $6.99 a month if you get government assistance

Amazon describes Prime Access as "a discounted version of the Prime membership" that "offers all the same benefits as the full-price Prime membership, but at half the cost—just $6.99/month." (Our math: $6.99 is $8 a month, about 53%, less than $14.99.) You qualify one of two ways: a third-party check that "your household income is at or below 200% of the Federal Poverty Guideline," or documents showing you're enrolled in a listed program. The list includes SSI, SNAP, Medicaid, TANF, WIC, LIHEAP, the National School Lunch Program, SSDI, the Direct Express card, Federal Public Housing Assistance and Veterans & Survivors Pension Benefits.

Unlike Prime for Young Adults, Prime Access members "can share certain benefits with other adults in your Amazon Family." New Prime Access members can "Start things off with a free 30-day trial."

Prime Video alone, Prime Video Ultra and the shipping-only plan

  • Prime Video ($8.99 a month) is the video service without shipping. Amazon's help page lists it as "Prime Video membership is $8.99 per month."
  • Prime Video Ultra ($4.99 a month) removes most ads and adds 4K. Amazon: "up to five concurrent streams (previously three), up to 100 downloads (previously 25), and exclusive access to 4K/UHD streaming." Regular Prime Video gets "4 streams (up from 3)" and "50 downloads for offline viewing (up from 25)." Ultra isn't fully ad-free: "Live TV and events, like sports, and other select ad-supported content and subscriptions may still include ads with Prime Video Ultra."
  • Prime Video + Ultra without Prime costs $8.99 + $4.99 = $13.98 a month (our math), just $1.01 less than full Prime with its ads.
  • Prime Shipping Only Plan ($14.99 a month, invitation only): Amazon says it "does not include any digital benefits, like Prime Video, and is available for a limited time only to eligible customers." It costs the same as full monthly Prime, so it isn't a cheaper way to get shipping.

Sharing Prime with your household

Prime members share through Amazon Family. Amazon's help page: "An Amazon Family can include two adults each with their own account, up to four teen accounts (added before April 7, 2025) or teen profiles, and up to four child profiles." Shared benefits include fast free delivery, "Prime Video (with ads)", Prime Reading, Amazon Photos and Amazon Music.

Three rules to know: "Amazon Family members must live together at the same primary residential address"; the organizer "must share a payment method for household verification. It can be used to make purchases within the Family"; and members who leave "must wait 12 months before joining a different Amazon Family."

Cancelling, pausing and refunds

Amazon's Prime Terms & Conditions ("Last updated August 14, 2026") set the refund rule: "If you cancel within 3 business days of signing up for or converting from a free trial to a paid membership, we will refund your full membership fee; provided that we may charge you (or withhold from your refund) the value of Prime benefits used by you and your account during this 3-business day period. If you cancel at any other time, we will refund your full membership fee only if you and your account did not make any eligible purchases or take advantage of Prime benefits since your latest Prime membership charge."

In plain terms: once you've used Prime in a billing period, cancelling won't get money back for that period under the Terms. (Amazon's country-switch help page does describe a pro-rata refund "based on the remaining (full) months" when members who used Prime cancel to switch countries, so if you're on an annual plan with months left, it's worth asking customer service.) Amazon's cancel page says refunds take "three to five business days" and that Prime Video add-on subscriptions "like Paramount+, will continue once your Prime Membership ends", so cancel those separately.

Pausing, from Amazon's pause page: "we won't bill you at the end of your current billing cycle," you lose benefits while paused, and you resume "at the then current rate." A pause of "more than 365 consecutive days" cancels the membership. You can't pause if you joined through a third party, have Prime Video only, Prime Business, Student Prime, a free trial or discounted offer, or the Prime Shipping only plan.

Joined through Google Play on Android? Amazon says to cancel "through Google Subscription services."

Will Amazon Prime go up in price?

Amazon hasn't announced a US increase. Its Terms promise notice first: "IF THE MEMBERSHIP FEE INCREASES, WE WILL NOTIFY YOU IN ADVANCE OF THE RENEWAL OF THE NEW FEE. YOU MAY CANCEL AT ANY TIME BEFORE THE RENEWAL TAKES EFFECT."

Analysts have expected one. In June 2025, J.P. Morgan said a 2026 increase would be "consistent with its cadence of raising the price every ~4 years," and modeled "a Prime price hike of $20 in the U.S." (Fox Business). That would put the yearly plan at $159 (our math). As of November 19, 2026, the four-year mark (February 2026) has passed with no US change.

Amazon Prime in Canada and Australia

Amazon's own Prime fee pages on November 19, 2026, in local currency:

  • Canada: "$9.99 per month" or "$99 per year"; Prime Student "$4.99 per month" or "$49 per year"; and a Prime Shipping Plan (invitation only) at "$9.99 per month."
  • Australia: "$99 for an annual membership or $12.99 per month with a monthly membership."

Amazon's $2.5 billion FTC settlement: you may be owed up to $200

In September 2025 Amazon settled the FTC's case over Prime sign-ups and cancellations, agreeing to "issue $1.5 billion in refunds and pay a $1 billion civil penalty" (CBS News). Under the revised order, you qualify if you signed up through one of the challenged sign-up pages or "tried to cancel a subscription but could not do so between June 23, 2019, and June 23, 2025," and "used no more than 20 Prime benefits, such as Prime Music or Prime Video, in any 12-month period" (Yahoo Finance).

You don't need to file anything: "Submitting a claim, completing a form, or responding to a notice are not required," and "payments expire 60 days after the issue date." No one gets "more than $200 in total" and, according to the court order, "The pro rata payments will begin by the end of April 2027." Watch for a PayPal, Venmo or check payment and accept it promptly.

What you can do

  1. Check your plan, price and renewal date on Amazon's Prime Membership page (Your Account > Prime). If you joined through Google Play or another company, they bill you and their rules apply.
  2. Pay yearly only if you'll keep Prime 10+ months. $139 a year beats $14.99 a month from the tenth month on. If you only want one sale, use the 30-day trial or one month and turn off renewal.
  3. Check the cheaper plans. Ages 18–24 or a college student: Prime for Young Adults ($7.49 or $69, 6-month trial). On SNAP, Medicaid, SSI or another listed program, or at or below 200% of the poverty line: Prime Access ($6.99).
  4. Share instead of paying twice. A second adult at the same address can share Prime through Amazon Family. Prime for Young Adults can't share.
  5. Decide whether you need Prime Video Ultra. It's $4.99 a month ($45.99 a year on annual Prime) and still shows ads on live sports.
  6. Pause rather than cancel if you'll be back: up to a year on Monthly or Annual Prime, resuming at the then-current price.
  7. Cancel straight after signing up if it was a mistake. The Terms promise a full refund within 3 business days, minus any benefits used, or a full refund later only if you haven't used Prime since the last charge.
  8. Watch for an FTC settlement payment if you signed up or couldn't cancel between June 23, 2019 and June 23, 2025. Payments expire after 60 days.
