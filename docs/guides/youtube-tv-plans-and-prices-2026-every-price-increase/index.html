YouTube TV price (November 2026): $82.99 main plan, the cheaper Sports and genre plans, NFL Sunday Ticket, add-ons and every price increase

Short answer: when we checked YouTube TV's plans page on November 20, 2026, the main YouTube TV plan (formerly the Base Plan) was $82.99 a month plus tax. Since February 2026 there are cheaper genre plans: Sports $64.99, Sports + News $71.99, News + Entertainment + Family $69.99, Entertainment $54.99 and more. New users can get the main plan for $59.99 a month for 2 months, or Sports + News for $49.99 for 6 months, until December 2, 2026. The main plan's price has risen from $35 at launch in 2017; the last increase was in January 2025.

Every plan gets the same features. YouTube TV's plans help page says: "All features that can be accessed on the YouTube TV main plan, will also be accessible on the other plans. The one thing you can't do: "you can't create your own plan by selecting individual channels."

Holiday 2026 offers: what new users get until December 2

YouTube TV has no separate Black Friday price for existing members. What it does have, for new users, are two offers on its welcome page that end on December 2, 2026:

  • Main plan: "New YouTube TV users pay $59.99/mo for 2 months of the main YouTube TV plan (save $46), then $82.99/mo thereafter." Our math: 2 x $23 off = $46, so your first year would cost $949.88 instead of $995.88.
  • Sports + News: "New YouTube TV users pay $49.99/mo for 6 months of Sports + News Plan (save $132), then $71.99/mo thereafter." Our math: $22 off for 6 months = $132; first-year total $731.88.
  • Cheaper genre plans: Entertainment ($44.99), News + Entertainment ($52.99) and News + Entertainment + Family ($59.99) are each "Save $30 total on your first 3 months"; Sports + Entertainment + Family is $62.99 for 3 months ("Save $45 total on your first 3 months").
  • Free trial: as of November 26, 2026 every plan shows a "2-day free trial". On November 20 most plans showed a "10-day free trial" and on November 22 to 25 a "5-day free trial" (the Sports Plan was already 2 days). Details.

Read the small print before you take one. The offers say "No refunds. Cancel anytime." and the plans help page warns: "If you are on a promotional price and switch plans, you will lose the promotion." YouTube TV's cancel page adds that if you come back later, "you may not be eligible for promotional prices".

Is the Sports Plan worth it? Main plan vs Sports vs Entertainment

YouTube TV's help center describes the Sports Plan as "a lower-priced option that only includes all the sports channels from the main plan, plus your local channels." When it launched, YouTube's blog said it was "$18 lower than the YouTube TV main plan" and includes "all the major broadcasters as well as sports networks like FS1, NBC Sports Network, all of the ESPN networks, and ESPN Unlimited (coming this fall)."

Our math at regular prices: the main plan is $995.88 a year. Sports is $779.88 ($216 less), Sports + News is $863.88 ($132 less) and Entertainment is $659.88 ($336 less). If you mostly watch games and your local channels, Sports saves the most without giving up local CBS, FOX, NBC and ABC; if you never watch sports, Entertainment is the cheapest plan with local channels.

Switching is allowed at any time, but there are rules (from YouTube TV's plans help page):

  • "When you switch your plan, your new plan starts right away." You're charged the new plan "minus a credit for the unused time remaining on your old plan", and that day becomes your new billing date.
  • Moving to a cheaper plan: "you will get a credit for the difference on your next payment."
  • Recordings follow your channels. If you drop a genre, its recordings disappear from your Library, but "as long as you re-upgrade their subscription to include that genre within 21 days, you will regain access to lost DVR content."
  • "Plan switching is also not available on the iOS or Android apps." Use a web browser. If a mobile carrier, internet provider or Google Play bills you, some plans may not be offered yet; YouTube's fix is to cancel there and "resubscribe directly through the YouTube TV homepage".

Add-on prices: 4K Plus, NFL RedZone, Entertainment Plus, Spanish Plus

These are the add-on prices on YouTube TV's welcome page on November 20, 2026 ("Terms and conditions apply. Prices vary."):

  • 4K Plus: $9.99 a month. "unlock unlimited simultaneous streams at home, the ability to watch recordings offline, and a 4K viewing experience on select content." It was $19.99 until March 2023, when YouTube TV cut it to $9.99 (TechCrunch).
  • NFL RedZone with Sports Plus: $10.99 a month. The help page says it is "a package of channels that you can add to your YouTube TV plan for $10.99 per month"; the channels in it "are not available as individual add-ons."
  • Entertainment Plus: $29.99 a month for "HBO Max™, Paramount+ with SHOWTIME, and STARZ© all in 1 place and for 1 great price".
  • Spanish Plus: $14.99 a month on top of a YouTube TV plan, with "25+ additional Spanish-language networks." If you only want Spanish-language TV, the standalone Spanish Plan ($24.99) is cheaper than any English plan.
  • Premium channels such as ALLBLK, MGM+ and Shudder: "From $1.99/mo".

Add-ons are billed pro rata. YouTube TV's help page: "you'll be charged a prorated amount for the time between when you added the network and your next billing date." Add-ons carry over when you switch plans, but "If you cancel your YouTube TV Plan then all add-ons will automatically be cancelled as well."

NFL Sunday Ticket 2026: the $48 rest-of-season sale ends November 30

NFL Sunday Ticket (out-of-market Sunday afternoon games) is sold per season on top of a YouTube TV plan. YouTube's Sunday Ticket page on November 20, 2026: "For new users, NFL Sunday Ticket with a YouTube TV Plan is $48 or 4 non-cancelable payments of $12/mo (1st charged at purchase). Returning users pay $62 or 4 non-cancelable payments of $15.50/mo (1st charged at purchase). Sale ends 11/30/26. Auto-renews at full price."

That is a late-season price. The full 2026 season price for returning subscribers was $378 with YouTube TV or $480 through YouTube Primetime Channels without YouTube TV (9to5Google); new subscribers who bought early were offered $192 (Cord Cutters News, February 2026). Three catches from YouTube's own terms:

  • "Auto-renews at full price." Turn off auto-renew if you only want the rest of 2026; 9to5Google notes memberships usually renew in August.
  • "No refunds." The payments are "non-cancelable".
  • "If you cancel YouTube TV, you'll lose access to NFL Sunday Ticket." Sunday Ticket needs an active YouTube TV plan.

How to pause or cancel YouTube TV

You can pause instead of cancelling. From YouTube TV's help page: "When you pause your membership, you can choose the length of this paused state, from 4 weeks up to 6 months." Other rules:

  • "Your membership will be paused after the end of your current billing cycle."
  • While paused, YouTube TV can't be watched and nothing new records. Your recordings are kept, though "recordings are still subject to our standard 9-month expiration date".
  • "When your paused state ends, you'll automatically be charged at your normal monthly price, and that date becomes your new billing date." You can resume early at any time.
  • To pause or cancel: on tv.youtube.com, select your profile photo, then Settings > Membership > Manage.

If you cancel: "you can still access YouTube TV until the end of your payment or free trial period." After that, "Recorded programs in your library will expire after 21 days." Pausing keeps your recordings longer, and it also keeps your add-ons, which a cancellation removes.

Our math: the main plan costs $47.99 a month more than at launch, up 137%. The individual steps were +14% (2018), +25% (2019), +30% (2020), +12% (2023) and +14% (2025). The 2023 increase came "after 3 years", as YouTube TV put it in its announcement; the 2025 one came less than two years later.

Will it go up again? YouTube TV hasn't announced a 2027 price. Its past increases were explained by rising content costs: in December 2024 Google's email to members said it needed to "keep up with the rising cost of content and the investments we make in the quality of our service" (9to5Google). Existing members have usually been given about a month's notice before a new price reaches their bill.

The 2025 Disney blackout and the $20 credit

From October 30 to November 14, 2025, Disney channels including ABC and ESPN were off YouTube TV during a carriage dispute. YouTube's statement on October 30 said: "If their content remains off YouTube TV for an extended period of time, we'll offer subscribers a $20 credit." 9to5Google reported on November 9, 2025 that the credit was being sent ("one-time $20.00 credit"). On November 14, 2025 Disney announced a multi-year deal, including "ESPN's new direct-to-consumer service (Unlimited Plan) to be made available at no additional cost to YouTube TV subscribers" and "Select networks to be included in various genre-specific packages". The genre plans launched in February 2026.

YouTube TV vs YouTube Premium: two separate bills

YouTube TV is live TV; YouTube Premium removes ads from regular YouTube and includes YouTube Music. Neither includes the other. Our math with our November 2026 price checks: the YouTube TV main plan ($82.99) plus YouTube Premium Individual ($15.99) is $98.98 a month. See our YouTube Premium price guide for Premium's plans and history, and our Hulu price guide for Hulu + Live TV and the Disney bundles.

Checklist: pay less for YouTube TV

  1. Check your plan and price at tv.youtube.com under Settings > Membership. If a carrier, internet provider or Google Play bills you, their rules (and plan list) apply.
  2. Price the genre plans. Sports ($64.99) saves $18 a month; Entertainment ($54.99) saves $28 a month. All plans keep unlimited DVR, 6 accounts and 3 streams.
  3. New user? Sign up before December 2 for $59.99 x 2 on the main plan or $49.99 x 6 on Sports + News, and don't switch plans while the promo runs.
  4. Sunday Ticket for the rest of 2026: $48 new or $62 returning until November 30, then turn off auto-renew if you don't want a full-price renewal.
  5. Pause, don't cancel, in the off-season: 4 weeks to 6 months, recordings and add-ons kept.
  6. Drop 4K Plus or RedZone when the season ends; add-ons are monthly and prorated.
  7. Compare a skinnier bundle: Sling Orange & Blue is $60.99 a month before local-channel fees, and Sling Essentials has ESPN for $19.99, but Sling carries locals only in some big markets. See our Sling TV price guide.
