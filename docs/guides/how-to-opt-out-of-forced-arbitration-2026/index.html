How to opt out of arbitration (2026): opt-out deadlines at Pinterest, Grindr, DoorDash, AT&T and more

Short answer: many companies give you 30 or 31 days to opt out of arbitration, usually by email or mail, starting when you sign up or when you're notified of new terms. Some offer none. Here is what each company's terms say, with the dates that matter now.

Published October 30, 2026

How arbitration opt-outs work

A forced-arbitration clause sends disputes to a private arbitrator instead of a court, usually one person at a time, with no class action. Some companies let you opt out, but only inside a short window and only by the method the terms name. Three things to check:

  • When the clock starts: when you first sign up, when new terms take effect, or when the company notifies you of a change.
  • How to send it: a specific email address, or a signed letter by mail. Other methods may not count.
  • What it covers: opting out of an update often keeps the *previous* arbitration agreement rather than getting you out of arbitration altogether.

Company by company

From the terms quoted in our coverage, with the date that matters for each.

  • Pinterest: opt-out added, 30 days. The new terms take effect November 12, 2026: "You may opt out of this Section 11 within 30 days of first becoming subject to it by emailing litigation@pinterest.com with your name, account email, and a statement that you're opting out." For existing users, the safest window is November 12 to December 12, 2026. The current terms (effective April 30, 2025) have no opt-out. Our article
  • Grindr: 31 days. New terms effective "The earlier of October 27, 2026 or user acceptance." Existing users can opt out of a material amendment with a notice "received within thirty-one (31) days after we notify you of the amendment" (Grindr's help-center notice was posted October 27). New users get 31 days after first accepting the terms. Email your legal name, mailing address, account email(s) and a copy of your signature to arbitrationoptout@grindr.com, yourself. Opting out of an amendment doesn't cancel an earlier arbitration agreement. Our article
  • DoorDash: new accounts only, 30 days. Terms effective October 16, 2026. Only people creating a DoorDash or Caviar account for the first time can opt out, by emailing opt-out@doordash.com within 30 days with your name and address, username, account email and a clear statement. It must come from you personally and cover only you. The terms say updates don't give existing customers a new chance to opt out. Our article
  • Evernote: 30 days, outside the EEA, UK and Switzerland. The October 1, 2026 terms take effect December 1, 2026 for accounts created before October 1. Email optout@evernote.com within 30 days of your first use after the arbitration agreement is introduced or updated. This matters most for users in Brazil, who move onto the arbitration terms on December 1. Users in the EEA, UK and Switzerland get Italian law and no arbitration agreement. Our article
  • Shein: 30 days. Written notice to us_legal@sheingroup.com within 30 days "after first becoming subject to this Arbitration Agreement," with your name, address, account email and an unequivocal statement. The October 21, 2026 update left this unchanged, and the terms say later changes don't reopen the window. Our article
  • AT&T: reject the change by mail, 30 days from first notice. New terms apply November 1, 2026. Mail your name, address, phone number, account number and a statement personally signed by you to Legal Department – Revised Arbitration Opt-Out, AT&T, 208 S. Akard, Office #2900.13, Dallas, Texas 75202. This keeps the prior version; it doesn't remove arbitration. If your first notice was AT&T's mid-September email, the window has likely closed. Our article
  • Cricket Wireless: same process, 30 days from Cricket's text. New terms apply December 1, 2026. Mail the same details to Legal Department – Revised Arbitration Opt-Out, Cricket, 208 S. Akard, Office #2900.13, Dallas, Texas 75202. If Cricket's text reached you around October 1, mail it by October 31. Use a certificate of mailing or tracking. Our article
  • Roblox: 30 days, by certified mail. Our article on the November 1, 2026 terms doesn't cover an opt-out, but the Roblox Terms of Use in our October 30 snapshot have one: within 30 days of first signing up, or within 30 days after Roblox gives notice of a material change. Send a notice titled "Arbitration Opt-Out Notice" by certified U.S. Mail or Federal Express to Roblox Corporation, Legal Department, 3150 S. Delaware St., San Mateo, CA 94403, with full name, Roblox username, email, a clear statement, and the user's signature (a parent or guardian's for a minor). For a material change, name the change. Our article
  • Canva: no opt-out found, and it reaches e-signature signers without an account. Canva's Terms of Use (effective October 30, 2026) require binding AAA arbitration under the Consumer Arbitration Rules, individual claims only, and a jury and class-action waiver; the only opt-out we found is for marketing messages. Canva's new Electronic Signature Feature Terms say that if you sign without a Canva account, "Canva's Terms of Use also apply." Our article
  • Oura: no opt-out. We checked the terms on October 17, 24 and 29: the only opt-outs are for marketing emails and texts. The new terms must be accepted in the app by November 4, 2026. See our Oura guide.
  • Xero: no opt-out found. We found no opt-out in the October 26, 2026 US arbitration terms, which apply to current customers from December 28, 2026. Claims must be brought within one year, and small claims court stays available after a Notice of Claim to legalnotices@xero.com and the 60-day period to meet and try to work it out. Our article

Also mentioned in our coverage

  • Discord: its terms let you decline arbitration by "emailing an opt-out notice to arbitration-opt-out@discord.com within 30 days" of its September 29, 2025 terms or of registering, whichever is later. For later changes to the arbitration agreement, the email must go before the 30-day period after the change expires (Discord terms, our October 30 snapshot).
  • WHOOP and Eight Sleep: both offered 30-day arbitration opt-outs when we compared them on October 17. WHOOP's terms say "unless you opt out of the agreement to arbitrate within 30 days" and let members reject an updated agreement by emailing support@whoop.com within 30 days; Eight Sleep says you may opt out "within 30 days of first agreeing" to its terms, by writing to legal@eightsleep.com.
  • Robinhood isn't an arbitration opt-out, but it's similar: from November 18, 2026, accounts are auto-enrolled in a Class Action Service that keeps 20% of payouts and stops you suing the defendants in cases where it files a claim for you. You can unenroll in the app or through Customer Service. Our article

How to send an opt-out so it counts

  1. Use exactly the address and method in the terms. DoorDash and Grindr say no other method works.
  2. Send it yourself, for your account only. DoorDash and Grindr say opt-outs sent by an agent or covering several users don't count.
  3. Include every item the terms list, usually your name, account email and a clear statement. Grindr also wants a mailing address and a copy of your signature; AT&T and Cricket want your account number and a signed statement.
  4. Keep proof: the sent email, or a certificate of mailing or tracking.
  5. Put the deadline in your calendar. Each of our articles has an "Add the date to your calendar" button. For anything not covered here, read the company's current terms; windows and addresses can change with each update.
