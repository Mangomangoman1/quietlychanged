AI and your data

Who trains on what you write, and who just promised not to.

Privacy policies are being rewritten around AI: what assistants remember, whether your files and messages can train models, and whether AI features can read data from other products.

Some changes here are good news, like a company closing a loophole in its no-training promise. Others widen what an AI feature is allowed to see. Each one is quoted word for word.

  • TermsVrewNotableNov 9

    Dec 1: one account per person, and AI will analyze project content and editing history until you delete the account

    From Dec 1, Vrew allows only one account per person (no team sharing on one login) and will use AI to analyze project content, assets and editing history for personalization, kept until the account is deleted. Object via chat support by Dec 1.

    deleted: The Service supports only one account per email address. Article 5, Paragraph 1 inserted: Only one account is permitted per User for the Service. Article 6, Paragraph 1
    Takes effect Dec 1 · in 22 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for Vrew
  • TermsWells FargoMajorNov 6

    Wells Fargo's Nov 24 Online Access Agreement: AI agents act as you, and you must prove you revoked them

    From Nov 24, whatever an AI or automation tool does in your Wells Fargo account counts as you doing it, and you stay responsible until you report revoking it and can show proof.

    inserted: "AI Agent" means any software, application, program, or other technology that takes autonomous, semi-autonomous, or non-autonomous action on behalf of any person or entity.
    Takes effect Nov 24 · in 15 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for Wells Fargo
  • TermsSmuleMajorNov 5

    Smule's Dec 4 terms: your recordings can train AI and be licensed to other companies, and under-18s are out

    From Dec 4, Smule may train AI on your recordings and license de-identified copies to other companies to build AI, old songs included. The opt-out only opens Dec 4.

    TO USE THE SERVICE, YOU MUST BE AT LEAST deleted: 13 inserted: 18 YEARS OF AGE
    Takes effect Dec 4 · in 25 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for Smule
  • PrivacyStripeNotableNov 3

    Stripe's Nov 20 privacy rewrite: AI voice agents, your data to your bank, and calls used to train AI

    Stripe's new privacy policy, effective Nov 20, lets AI voice agents call people and use the recordings to train AI, and says it may send your IP, email, phone and address to your card's bank.

    inserted: Stripe may also share fraud risk signals—including Stripe's risk assessment, and your IP address, email address, phone number, and shipping address—with your card-issuing bank
    Takes effect Nov 20 · in 11 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for Stripe
  • PrivacyCalmNotableNov 2

    Calm says it may now infer health traits from feelings you share, and adds AI terms

    Calm's Oct 13 privacy policy drops its promise not to infer health traits from feelings you share in messages. New terms cover AI features fed with your emotions and stress.

    inserted: Certain AI Features may allow you to submit information regarding your emotions, well-being, stress levels, personal experiences, goals, or other wellness-related matters.
    Read the change →Source ↗ for Calm
  • PrivacyTubiNotableNov 1

    Tubi moves onto Fox's privacy policy: it can now collect your precise location, and its no-targeted-ads age for teens drops from 18 to 16

    Tubi's Oct 22 privacy policy is Fox's joint policy: it allows precise location, adds AI tools for ads and support, and cuts a teen targeted-ads limit from under-18 to under-16.

    deleted: In certain circumstances, we also may collect general location information, such as the city, state, and country where the device is located. inserted: In certain circumstances, we also may collect geolocation data (including your precise location).
    Read the change →Source ↗ for Tubi
  • PrivacyToyotaNotableOct 31

    Toyota now lists training large language models as a use of your personal information, and adds Acxiom tracking across your devices

    Toyota's Oct 28 privacy notices add "training large language models" as a use of personal data, including connected-car data, plus Acxiom cross-device tracking and AI in customer support.

    inserted: For the purpose of training large language models;
    Read the change →Source ↗ for Toyota
  • TermsGrindrNotableOct 28

    Grindr's new terms: AI can summarize your chats for other users even if you don't use AI, and opting out won't lower your bill

    Grindr's new terms say its AI may generate summaries about you from your chats and show them to other users, whether or not you use AI. Turn those settings off and paid features may stop, with no change to your bill.

    inserted: If you turn off a setting that a Paid Feature depends on, that feature will stop working for you until you turn the setting back on. Your Subscription and its billing are not affected by your settings choices.
    Read the change →Source ↗ for Grindr
  • PrivacyTodoistNotableOct 24

    Privacy policy now says user data isn't used to train any AI models

    Todoist's privacy policy now says your data isn't used to train any AI models. It used to rule out only "generalized" ones.

    Read the change →Source ↗ for Todoist
  • TermsEvernoteMajorOct 18

    Contract moves to Italian affiliate Dec 1; Brazil users lose local law and get arbitration; no password-manager use

    On Dec 1 your Evernote contract moves to an Italian company, Brazil users get arbitration, and storing passwords or financial info is banned.

    inserted: you will not use the Evernote Service as a password manager or to store sensitive financial information.
    Takes effect for existing users Dec 1 · in 22 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for Evernote
  • PrivacyGoogleMajorOct 17

    Gmail, Photos and Drive data you share with Search or Gemini can now shape ads

    Google's promise not to use Gmail, Drive and Photos content for ads now has an exception: if you let Search or Gemini read that data, it can shape your ads.

    Read the change →Source ↗ for Google
