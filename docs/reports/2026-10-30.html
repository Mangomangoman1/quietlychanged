Canva adds e-signatures, and its new terms say it won't verify who signs

2 changes · checked October 30, 2026

What changed

  • TermsCanvaNotableOct 30

    Canva: Canva adds e-signatures, and its new terms say it doesn't verify who signs and isn't an archive for your signed documents

    Canva's updated Terms of Use (effective Oct 30) point to new e-signature terms: Canva doesn't check signers' identity, bans wills and powers of attorney, and says keep your own copy.

    These Terms of Use are effective as of deleted: 19 August inserted: 30 October 2026.

    Who it affects: Anyone who uses Canva, and especially anyone who sends or signs documents with Canva's electronic signature feature, including signers who don't have a Canva account.

    Took effect Oct 30 · todayRead the change →Source ↗ for Canva
  • TermsCash AppMinorOct 30

    Cash App: Cash App's saved checkout info now extends to loyalty programs and in-store Square checkout, and the Local Cash tip rule changes

    Cash App's terms rename "Checkout powered by Cash App" to "Buyer Features," adding loyalty and rewards programs and Square point-of-sale devices. A rule forcing tips onto Local Cash is gone.

    deleted: any tip you choose to apply for that transaction must also be paid with Local Cash. inserted: the amount of any tip you choose to apply for that transaction may be limited by the amount of Local Cash available for such tip.

    Who it affects: Cash App users who save checkout details, use Local Cash, or join loyalty programs at Square sellers.

    Read the change →Source ↗ for Cash App

Canva's new Terms of Use took effect on October 30 and point to new Electronic Signature Feature Terms. Under them, Canva doesn't verify any signer's identity, bans the feature for wills, trusts, powers of attorney and deeds, and says it isn't an archive, so download your signed copy. Cash App's terms now extend saved checkout details to loyalty programs and in-store Square point-of-sale devices, and drop a rule that made tips come out of Local Cash. The other diffs were noise or already covered. Anthropic's pricing FAQ added a line about negotiated discounts, Audible and Mailchimp served the other versions of the pricing pages they rotate between, and PayPal's privacy statement now hides its contact emails behind an "[email protected]" placeholder. Social Security's Client Data System notice, which we covered yesterday, was published in the Federal Register today. PlayStation's terms page failed to load today because of an SSL error.
