Cash App lets you stop subscription charges on its card and adds a bills tool; YouTube TV's free trial shrinks to 2 days

2 changes · checked November 26, 2026

What changed

  • TermsCash AppNotableNov 26

    Cash App: Cash App now lets you stop subscription charges on your Cash App Card, and its new bills tool may ask for your passwords

    Cash App's terms dropped "No Right to Stop Payment": you can now block recurring card charges 3 business days ahead. A new Bills and Subscriptions tool can act for you with power of attorney.

    inserted: Blocking a merchant does not automatically cancel underlying subscriptions or agreements with the merchant.

    Who it affects: Cash App Card holders with subscriptions or automatic payments on the card, and anyone who turns on Cash App's new Bills and Subscriptions feature

    Read the change →Source ↗ for Cash App
  • PricingYouTube TVNotableNov 26

    YouTube TV: YouTube TV's free trial is now 2 days on every plan, down from 5 (and 10 a week ago)

    YouTube TV's plans page now shows a "2-day free trial" on every plan, down from 5 days yesterday and 10 days on Nov 20. Your first charge comes days sooner.

    100+ channels deleted: 5-day inserted: 2-day free trial $82.99/mo

    Who it affects: People thinking of trying YouTube TV, including its main plan and genre plans; current members aren't affected

    Read the change →Source ↗ for YouTube TV

Cash App's Terms of Service ("Last Updated: November 25, 2026") replace "No Right to Stop Payment" with "Right to Stop Payment": you can now stop preauthorized (subscription and recurring) Cash App Card charges if Cash App gets the request at least three business days before the charge, by blocking the merchant in the Activity tab, through Cash App Support, or by calling (800) 969-1940. Blocking doesn't cancel the subscription itself. A new "Bills and Subscriptions" section adds an optional tool that can manage and cancel subscriptions for you; it may ask for your merchant usernames and passwords and gives Cash App and an outside vendor "a limited power of attorney". https://cash.app/legal/us/en-us/tos

YouTube TV's plans page now shows a "2-day free trial" on every plan, down from "5-day free trial" on November 22 to 25 and a "10-day free trial" on most plans when we checked on November 20. Prices and the new-user offers ending December 2 are unchanged. https://tv.youtube.com/learn/explore-plans/
