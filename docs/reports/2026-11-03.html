Social Security updates its SSN master file, adding Treasury "Do Not Pay" data sharing

2 changes · checked November 3, 2026

What changed

  • PrivacyStripeNotableNov 3

    Stripe: Stripe's Nov 20 privacy rewrite: AI voice agents, your data to your bank, and calls used to train AI

    Stripe's new privacy policy, effective Nov 20, lets AI voice agents call people and use the recordings to train AI, and says it may send your IP, email, phone and address to your card's bank.

    inserted: Stripe may also share fraud risk signals—including Stripe's risk assessment, and your IP address, email address, phone number, and shipping address—with your card-issuing bank

    Who it affects: Anyone who has paid through a Stripe-powered checkout or has a Link account, plus anyone Stripe contacts for sales or support.

    Takes effect Nov 20 · in 11 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for Stripe
  • PrivacySocial SecurityMinorNov 3

    Social Security: Social Security updates its SSN master file: new Treasury 'Do Not Pay' data sharing starts Dec 3

    SSA's file on everyone issued an SSN adds a routine use letting it share records with Treasury's Do Not Pay system to catch improper payments. Comments close Dec 3.

    inserted: We obtain information from this system from additional existing SSA systems of records, such as the Claims Folders System, 60-0089, and Master Beneficiary Record, 60-0090.

    Who it affects: Anyone who has applied for or been issued a Social Security number.

    Comments close Dec 3 · in 24 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for Social Security

Social Security's Nov 3 Federal Register notice modifies its Master Files of SSN Holders and SSN Applications (60-0058): a new routine use lets SSA share records with Treasury's Do Not Pay system from Dec 3, and the file now also draws on Federal contractors, vendors and other SSA systems. Comments close Dec 3.

The other diffs were noise: Slack dropped the "New" tag from Slackbot, Todoist added a Table layout row and renamed "Start for free" to "Create a free account" (plus a comment-marker cleanup in its privacy policy), 1Password replaced [PSA URL]/[LINK] placeholders in its business terms with real links, Robinhood's customer agreement only reshuffled who delivers statements (a Robinhood Financial/Securities wording tidy) and added short-stock collateral to the High-Yield Cash Program interest line, Verizon's regulatory-charge note changed from "will increase" to "increased" for the already-covered $0.21 to $0.26 rise, Audible and Mailchimp served rotating pricing-page variants, Toyota relabeled chart headings, Cloudflare and Tubi had whitespace and device-ID noise.
