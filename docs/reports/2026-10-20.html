Proton downgraded its deletion warnings for inactive free accounts to "a mere courtesy"

6 changes · checked October 20, 2026

What changed

  • TermsPinterestMajorOct 20

    Pinterest: Nov 12 terms: mass claims go to one arbitrator, ads follow you to your TV, and a 30-day opt-out

    From Nov 12, 25 or more similar claims against Pinterest go to one arbitrator, and its ads can follow you to your TV. You get a 30-day window to opt out of arbitration.

    deleted: one arbitrator to resolve each batch inserted: all of those Claims will be consolidated into one arbitration before one arbitrator

    Who it affects: Pinterest users in the U.S. and everywhere outside the EEA, Switzerland and the UK (the arbitration section doesn't apply to consumers there), plus any Pinterest user who sees ads on other sites, apps or connected TVs, or who sends messages on Pinterest

    New terms take effect Nov 12 · in 14 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for Pinterest
  • TermsProtonNotableOct 20

    Proton: Inactive free accounts: deletion warnings are now only a "courtesy"

    Proton used to promise warnings before deleting inactive free accounts. Now the warnings are "a mere courtesy" it will "aim" to send.

    Who it affects: Anyone with a free Proton Mail, Drive, Calendar or Pass account they rarely use, especially backup or secondary accounts, and anyone whose recovery email is missing or out of date

    Read the change →Source ↗ for Proton
  • TermsGoogle PlayNotableOct 20

    Google Play: Nov 17 terms: report broken purchases to the developer, not Google; Play services now covered

    From Nov 17, Google Play's terms tell you to report broken purchases to the developer instead of Google, and drop "defective" content as a refund example.

    notify deleted: us or the Provider

    Who it affects: Everyone who uses the Google Play Store or an Android device with Google Play services, especially people who buy apps, games, movies, books or subscriptions on Google Play

    New terms take effect Nov 17 · in 19 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for Google Play
  • TermsIKEANotableOct 20

    IKEA: Gift Registry shuts down after Dec 27, 2026, and saved registries will be deleted

    IKEA's U.S. gift registry closes after Dec 27, and saved registries are deleted, not moved anywhere.

    inserted: After December 27th, 2026, IKEA Gift Registry will no longer be available.

    Who it affects: Anyone with an IKEA Gift Registry in the U.S. or Canada, especially couples and parents-to-be with events after December 27, 2026, and the friends and family shopping from those registries

    Registry shuts down Dec 27 · in 59 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for IKEA
  • PricingSpotifyMinorOct 20

    Spotify: Premium free trial doubled to 2 months for new users, until November 1

    New Spotify Premium Individual subscribers get two free months instead of one, until Nov 1. Then it's $12.99 a month.

    Try deleted: 1 month inserted: 2 months for $0

    Who it affects: People in the US who have never had Spotify Premium; past or current subscribers don't qualify

    Offer ends Nov 1 · in 3 daysRead the change →Source ↗ for Spotify
  • PricingSlackMinorOct 20

    Slack: New "Frontline teams" add-on for Enterprise+

    Slack lists a new "Frontline teams" add-on for Enterprise+, with no price shown.

    inserted: Enterprise+ add-on giving field workers Slack for task management, agentic support & secure Salesforce access.

    Who it affects: Companies on Slack Enterprise+ with field, retail or other frontline staff

    Read the change →Source ↗ for Slack

Three real updates out of twelve raw page diffs. Proton's terms used to promise warnings 30, 15 and 7 days before deleting a free account that's been inactive for 12 months; now it only "will aim to" send them, "as a mere courtesy," and the page's "Last modified" date still reads October 9. Spotify doubled its US Premium free trial to two months for first-time users, until November 1, and Slack added a new Frontline teams add-on for Enterprise+. Bank of America's new online banking agreement, which we covered on October 15, took effect on October 19. Shopify's yearly prices showed $89 and $359 again, the same rotating test we reported yesterday. The rest was noise: Tinder and Audible served Spanish-language copies, PayPal's page hid email addresses, Squarespace relabeled its buttons with no price change, and Canva and Grammarly made tiny wording edits. Amazon Prime's help pages failed to load.
