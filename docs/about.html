About QuietlyChanged

Companies revise their prices, plan limits, terms of service and privacy policies all the time, usually without announcement. QuietlyChanged keeps a public, dated record of those changes, with the company's own words and a link to the source.

Who we are

QuietlyChanged is a small independent publication that tracks the fine print of popular services. Questions, tips and corrections go to quietlychangedbiz@gmail.com; see Contact & corrections.

How a change gets published

  1. Snapshot (automatic). Every morning software fetches the public pricing, terms and privacy pages of every tracked service and saves the main text.
  2. Compare (automatic). Each snapshot is compared line by line with the previous day's, filtering out noise such as cookie banners, scripts, timestamps and copyright lines.
  3. Draft. Meaningful differences are written up in plain English: what changed, who it affects, any date you have to act by, and a link to the source.
  4. Review (a person). A person reviews each change before it is published: the quoted wording is checked against the source page, and dates and prices are checked against the documents they come from.
  5. Publish. The change gets its own page, goes into the day's report and the archive, and onto the page for that service.

How we source

  • Official pages, snapshotted and linked. Every change links the company page it was found on. We keep our own dated copy of the text, so a change can be shown even after the page moves on.
  • Quotes from the source. Text set in a typewriter face is the company's exact wording, never a paraphrase.
  • Before and after. Each change opens with a blackline of the words removed and added, and most include the full diff between our two snapshots.
  • Research, labeled. Some changes are confirmed in a company's help pages, announcements or filings rather than our snapshots. Those excerpts are marked "Quoted from the documents," and the documents are linked. Price histories list their sources under every table.

Reading a blackline

Lawyers compare two versions of a contract with a blackline, and so do we. Removed words are struck through in red; added words are underlined in blue.

"From our snapshot diff" means the words were taken directly from our before-and-after copies. "Quoted from the documents" means the change was confirmed in the company's own documents or announcements, linked from the article.

Impact ratings

Major changes what you pay, what you can do, or what happens to your data, often with a deadline. Notable worth knowing if you use the service. Minor small edits: new features, relabeled plans, wording cleanups.

Categories

Pricing prices, plans, limits, and fees. Terms terms of service, user agreements, and contracts of carriage. Privacy privacy policies and data-use notices.

Threads, deadlines and guides

Threads connect the same kind of change across companies, like forced arbitration or AI training. Due soon lists every date we find in a change, with calendar files. Guides walk through what you can do about the changes that ask something of you.

Corrections

If we get something wrong, we fix it and add a dated correction note to the article. Headlines and links stay the same, so nothing you've shared breaks. How to report an error: Contact & corrections.

Limits

Some sites block automated readers or load their text with scripts; we only track pages we can read reliably. Summaries are not legal or financial advice. Always check the linked source.
