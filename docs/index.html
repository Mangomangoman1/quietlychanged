Proton downgraded its deletion warnings for inactive free accounts to "a mere courtesy"

October 20, 2026 · 3 changes

What changed

  • Terms

    Proton: Inactive free accounts: deletion warnings are now only a "courtesy"

    What changed
    Proton edited the inactive-accounts section of its Terms of Service, and the page still says "Last modified: October 09, 2026." Free accounts inactive for 12 months or more can have the account and some or all of its data deleted, including Proton Mail emails, Proton Drive files, Proton Calendar entries and Proton Pass passwords. Until yesterday the terms promised: "You will receive notices 30, 15 and 7 days in advance before such action is taken on the recovery email associated with your Account." As of October 20, 2026 that sentence reads: "As a mere courtesy, the Company will aim to send notice to the recovery email associated with your Account 30, 15 and 7 days in advance before such action is taken." The warnings went from a promise to a best effort. Paid accounts are still always treated as active, and logging in on the web, desktop or mobile apps at least once every 12 months still counts as activity.
    Who it affects
    Anyone with a free Proton Mail, Drive, Calendar or Pass account they rarely use, especially backup or secondary accounts, and anyone whose recovery email is missing or out of date

  • Pricing

    Spotify: Premium free trial doubled to 2 months for new users, until November 1

    What changed
    Spotify's US Premium page changed its free trial from "Try 1 month for $0" to "Try 2 months for $0" on October 20, 2026. The offer covers Premium Individual only and is "only available if you haven't tried Premium before." After the two free months it costs $12.99 per month. The page now adds "Offer ends November 1, 2026."
    Who it affects
    People in the US who have never had Spotify Premium; past or current subscribers don't qualify
    October 20, 2026View source page for Spotify →
  • Pricing

    Slack: New "Frontline teams" add-on for Enterprise+

    What changed
    Slack's pricing page now lists a new "Frontline teams" add-on, marked "New." Slack describes it as an "Enterprise+ add-on giving field workers Slack for task management, agentic support & secure Salesforce access." It appears in the feature list of every plan and in the comparison table as an add-on. The page shows no price for it.
    Who it affects
    Companies on Slack Enterprise+ with field, retail or other frontline staff
    October 20, 2026View source page for Slack →

Three real updates out of twelve raw page diffs. Proton's terms used to promise warnings 30, 15 and 7 days before deleting a free account that's been inactive for 12 months; now it only "will aim to" send them, "as a mere courtesy," and the page's "Last modified" date still reads October 9. Spotify doubled its US Premium free trial to two months for first-time users, until November 1, and Slack added a new Frontline teams add-on for Enterprise+. Bank of America's new online banking agreement, which we covered on October 15, took effect on October 19. Shopify's yearly prices showed $89 and $359 again, the same rotating test we reported yesterday. The rest was noise: Tinder and Audible served Spanish-language copies, PayPal's page hid email addresses, Squarespace relabeled its buttons with no price change, and Canva and Grammarly made tiny wording edits. Amazon Prime's help pages failed to load.
