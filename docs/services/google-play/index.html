Changelog

  • Terms

    Nov 17 terms: report broken purchases to the developer, not Google; Play services now covered

    Google is replacing the Google Play Terms of Service on November 17, 2026. The current terms page, dated July 29, 2026, now says: "We're updating our Play Terms of Service on November 17, 2026. Until then, the Terms below continue to apply." It links to a preview dated November 17, 2026. According to Open Terms Archive's captures, the notice first appeared around October 5 with a November 5 date and was changed to November 17 on October 20. We compared the preview with the current terms line by line. The edits are small, and most of them shift responsibility away from Google. Defective purchases: today the terms tell you to check what you bought and "notify us or the Provider as soon as reasonably possible if you find any errors or defect." The new version says to "notify the Provider," so Google drops itself as a contact. In the cancellation clause, the line that you won't get a refund for the current billing period "except as otherwise provided in Google Play's Refund Policy (for example where Content is defective)" loses its "(for example where Content is defective)" example. Scope: the "Content" the Play terms cover now includes "system services (including, but not limited to, those provided by Google Play services)," in addition to the apps, games, books and other content "available through the Play Store." So the rules for Content now apply to Google's background system software too. The July 29 update already made you responsible for carrier data charges from system services, including in the background when the screen is locked. Free trials: the trial clause now covers access "at no charge for a specified trial period" and drops the words "or at a reduced price," so discounted intro offers are no longer described as trials there. Taxes can now be charged if "the Provider or Google or its affiliates" must collect them (it used to say "the seller of Content or Google"). "Google may allow you to download, view or use Content free of charge" becomes "You may be permitted to." Unchanged: at least 30 days' notice of terms changes, at least 30 days' notice before a subscription price increase applies, and charges up to 48 hours before each billing period. Coverage (checked October 20): ConductAtlas logged the November 5 notice on October 6, and Android Authority covered the July 29 update. We found no reporting on what the November 17 version changes. What you can do: if something you bought on Google Play doesn't work, report it to the developer and also request a refund through Google Play's refund process, keeping records of both. Under the new wording, the developer is the party you're told to notify.
