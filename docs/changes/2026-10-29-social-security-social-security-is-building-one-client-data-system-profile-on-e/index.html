SSA Client Data System (60-0392): new Social Security profile on every SSN holder, comments due Nov 29, 2026

A new SSA records system keeps one profile per SSN: citizenship, earnings, benefits, marriage history, call notes, alert flags. Email may be used for surveys. Comments close Nov 29.

Comments close Nov 29in 31 days

struck removed underlined added

What changed

The Social Security Administration (SSA) is creating a new Privacy Act system of records called the Client Data System (60-0392). The notice is on public inspection now and is scheduled to be published in the Federal Register on October 30, 2026 (FR Doc. 2026-22173, docket SSA-2025-0030). SSA says it will "track and manage information about a customer including personal information and customer interactions" and give employees "one main location to view information concerning the customer."

The notice lists no history (it is a new system) and claims no Privacy Act exemptions.

Change 1: one profile for everyone with an SSN

The notice says the system "maintains information on individuals who have been issued a Social Security number (SSN), including those who have conducted business with SSA beyond enumeration." Records are retrieved "by the number holder's SSN." That covers anyone who has ever been issued a number, not only people who receive benefits. For comparison, SSA's existing customer-service records system (Appointments, Visitor Information, and Customer Service Record System, 60-0350, republished August 20, 2025) covers "individuals who visit an SSA office" or make an appointment.

Change 2: what goes into it

The new system pulls data "primarily from existing SSA systems of records," and the notice names 12 of them, including the Master Files of SSN Holders (60-0058), the Earnings Recording system (60-0059), Claims Folders (60-0089), the Master Beneficiary Record (60-0090), the SSI record (60-0103), the Medicare Database File (60-0321), the Digital Identity File System (60-0373) and the Customer Engagement Tools system (60-0383). It also takes data from "third parties such as a contractor or vendor; and Federal, State, and local agencies."

The record categories in one place include: name, SSN, sex, date of birth, parent names and place of birth; "country of citizenship"; mailing and email address, phone, language and texting opt-ins; "customer interaction history, including notes or remarks entered by technicians"; representative payee and appointed representative details, including fee agreements; benefit and SSI payment amounts; "yearly earnings and quarters of coverage"; accommodation needs such as Braille, interpreters or hearing loops; marriage details including the spouse's SSN, "reason for marriage end" and "marriage type"; "electronic block, high-risk, and special indicator warnings"; and indicators for the Defense Department's Wounded Warrior program and a VA "100% Permanent and Total Disability Compensation Rating."

Change 3: a new use for your email address

The notice adds: "We may also use contact information (e.g., email addresses) provided by individuals who conduct business with SSA for program outreach (e.g., notification about our programs, online services, SSA events, and surveys) and other purposes related to our administration of the Social Security Act." We did not find that outreach wording in the source notices we checked (60-0350, 60-0383 and 60-0058). Other listed purposes include tracking "consent to text," identity verification, "prevention of fraud," research, staffing decisions and "agency strategic goals and funding decisions."

Change 4: who it can be shared with

The notice lists 30 routine uses, which take effect 30 days after publication. Most match uses already in SSA's other notices. They include the Department of Homeland Security "upon request, to identify and locate aliens in the United States" under 8 U.S.C. 1360(b) (SSA's Master Files notice, 60-0058, already has this use); "Federal, State, and local law enforcement agencies and private security contractors" for office safety and related investigations; law enforcement investigating criminal violations of the Social Security Act; "private collection under contract with us" to recover overpayments; Treasury's Do Not Pay system; state agencies running Medicaid, TANF and food stamps (SNAP); contractors, other agencies and researchers under written agreements; and employers and private pension plans in situations where a person's whereabouts are unknown.

SSA says it will not disclose IRS "return or return information" unless authorized. Is this the first shared SSA client database? No. A 2007 SSA privacy impact assessment describes an "Integrated Client Database (ICDB) System" that already shared "Client Data" (names, SSNs, dates of birth, sex, language preference, "their country of citizenship and the basis of U.S. citizenship") among SSA's claims systems, including for people not getting benefits.

At the time SSA wrote that ICDB "does not require a new Privacy Act system of records" because the data was covered by existing notices such as 60-0058, 60-0090 and 60-0103. The new notice is different in three ways: it gives the combined profile its own system of records, its record list goes well beyond the identity fields the 2007 assessment names (interaction notes, marriage details, earnings, payment amounts and alert flags), and it states the email outreach and survey purpose.

What stays the same

SSA says the data comes mainly from systems it already keeps, so the notice doesn't describe new information being collected from you. What changes is that it all sits in one SSN-indexed profile, along with interaction notes and alert flags that until now were kept in separate systems. You can't opt out of a system of records. You can ask for your record or contest it, but the notice says you "may need to identify each system of records in which the individual seeks to correct or amend the record" and follow each one's procedure.

What you can do

Comment by November 29, 2026 (30 days after publication) at regulations.gov under docket SSA-2025-0030, or by mail to the Head of Privacy and Disclosure Policy, Law and Policy, SSA, Room G-401 West High Rise, 6401 Security Boulevard, Baltimore, MD 21235-6401. Questions go to LP.PDP.SORN@ssa.gov or (410) 965-1727. If you'd rather not get outreach email or texts, check the contact and messaging preferences in your my Social Security account.

To see what SSA holds on you, send a written request with your name and SSN to the system manager, with a notarized statement or a signed certification of identity.

Who it affects

Anyone who has been issued a Social Security number, including people who have never claimed benefits. Retirees, SSI recipients, people with representative payees or appointed representatives, and anyone who has contacted SSA have the most data in it.

Quick answers

What is the SSA Client Data System?

A new Social Security Administration system of records (60-0392), announced in a Privacy Act notice scheduled for the October 30, 2026 Federal Register (FR Doc. 2026-22173). It gives SSA employees "one main location to view information concerning the customer," built mainly from 12 existing SSA record systems.

Who is in it?

The notice says it covers "individuals who have been issued a Social Security number (SSN), including those who have conducted business with SSA beyond enumeration." Records are retrieved by SSN.

What information does it hold?

Identity data (name, SSN, sex, date of birth, parents' names, place of birth), country of citizenship, contact details and texting opt-ins, interaction history including technicians' notes, representative payee and appointed representative details, benefit and SSI payment amounts, yearly earnings, accommodation needs, marriage details including the spouse's SSN and why a marriage ended, high-risk and other alert flags, and Wounded Warrior and VA 100% disability indicators.

Can SSA share it with DHS or law enforcement?

Yes, under its routine uses. The list includes DHS "upon request, to identify and locate aliens in the United States" under 8 U.S.C. 1360(b), a use SSA's Master Files notice (60-0058) already includes, plus law enforcement and private security contractors for office safety, criminal investigations of Social Security Act violations, private collection contractors for overpayments, Treasury's Do Not Pay system and state benefit agencies. The routine uses take effect 30 days after publication.

Will SSA email me?

It may. The notice says SSA may use email addresses and other contact information "for program outreach (e.g., notification about our programs, online services, SSA events, and surveys)." The system also tracks texting opt-ins and opt-outs.

Didn't SSA already have a shared client database?

Yes, for basic identity data. SSA's 2007 privacy impact assessment for its Integrated Client Database (ICDB) describes shared "Client Data" such as names, SSNs, dates of birth, sex, language preference and "country of citizenship and the basis of U.S. citizenship," and says ICDB "does not require a new Privacy Act system of records." The Client Data System gets its own notice, lists far more than those identity fields (interaction notes, marriage details, earnings, payment amounts, alert flags) and states the email outreach purpose.

Can I opt out or comment?

You can't opt out of a system of records. You can comment until November 29, 2026 (30 days after the October 30 publication). Online: go to regulations.gov, search docket SSA-2025-0030 (the docket is expected to open there once the notice publishes), choose the Client Data System notice and click Comment. Comments are public. By mail: Head of Privacy and Disclosure Policy, Law and Policy, SSA, Room G-401 West High Rise, 6401 Security Boulevard, Baltimore, MD 21235-6401. SSA invites comments on "the routine uses or other aspects of this SORN." You can also ask SSA for your record or contest it under its Privacy Act rules.
