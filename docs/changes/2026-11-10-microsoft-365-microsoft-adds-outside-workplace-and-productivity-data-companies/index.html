Microsoft privacy statement update: workplace data sources, logged-out age checks (Nov 2026)

Microsoft's privacy statement now says it gets data from companies that supply workplace and productivity data for product research, and may use device info to apply age protections when you aren't signed in.

The wording

struck removed underlined added

What changed

Microsoft revised its privacy statement. We compared the Nov 10 capture with Nov 9.

Change 1: A new source of personal data

The list of sources Microsoft collects data from (which already included "Publicly available sources, such as open public sector, academic, and commercial data sets") gains a new line: "Companies that provide workplace and productivity data for research, development, and improvement of our products and services." The statement doesn't name those companies.

Change 2: Age protections without signing in

The age-appropriate experiences section, which already covered age range signals from app stores and sharing age group information with third-party apps, adds: "In services you can use without logging in, we may use device-based information to help us apply age-appropriate protections."

Change 3: More device data named

Device data examples now read: "IP addresses, age of the device, device identifiers and type, device language and locale settings, nearby networks," adding device age, type and language settings.

Change 4: Personalized offers off still uses some data

A new paragraph says that if you turn off Personalized offers in Windows, Microsoft "may use basic account data and contextual info about your device" to avoid irrelevant tips and ads, for example "your active subscriptions to avoid showing you ads for products you have already purchased."

Change 5: Phone Link rewritten, location sharing added

The separate Android and iPhone sections of Phone Link were merged into one "linked mobile experiences" section that also covers apps such as WhatsApp. It adds a Location sharing feature: "You can access location information from your mobile device after you choose to enable this feature." Microsoft still says it does not store the content of your texts, call history, photos, files or notifications on its servers, except where a feature says otherwise.

The page does not state a new effective date in our capture.

Who it affects

Anyone with a Microsoft account or Windows PC, including people who use Microsoft services without signing in and Phone Link users

Source

Detected on the Microsoft 365 privacy page: https://privacy.microsoft.com/en-us/privacystatement. Always check the source; this summary is not legal or financial advice.
