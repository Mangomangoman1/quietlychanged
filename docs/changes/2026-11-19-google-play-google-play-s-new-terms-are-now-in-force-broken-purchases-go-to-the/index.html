Google Play Terms of Service took effect Nov 17, 2026: defective purchases, Play services, trials

The Google Play Terms of Service previewed in October took effect Nov 17. You're now told to report defective purchases to the developer, not Google.

Took effect Nov 174 days ago

The wording

struck removed underlined added

What changed

The Google Play Terms of Service we covered on October 20 are now the live version. Our November 16 snapshot of the US terms page still showed "We’re updating our Play Terms of Service on November 17, 2026. Until then, the Terms below continue to apply" and a link to the preview; by November 19 that notice was gone and the page carries the new text.

The live terms match the preview. On defective purchases you're told to check Content and "notify the Provider as soon as reasonably possible if you find any errors or defect" (it used to say "notify us or the Provider"). The cancellation clause no longer gives "(for example where Content is defective)" as an example of when the Refund Policy allows a refund. "Content" now includes "system services (including, but not limited to, those provided by Google Play services)", and the trial clause no longer mentions access "at a reduced price". The Google ToS quote on third-party rights is updated to the current Google Terms wording.

What stays the same: at least 30 days' notice of terms changes and before a subscription price increase applies.

Who it affects

Everyone who uses the Google Play Store or an Android device with Google Play services

Detected on the Google Play terms page: https://play.google.com/intl/en_us/about/play-terms/index.html. Always check the source; this summary is not legal or financial advice.
