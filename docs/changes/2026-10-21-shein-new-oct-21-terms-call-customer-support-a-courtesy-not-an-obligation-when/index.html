Shein terms change Oct 21, 2026: customer support is now "a courtesy," not an obligation, for carrier, bank and seller problems

October 21, 2026 · Detected by QuietlyChanged's daily check

Shein updated its U.S. Terms of Use today. The top of the page now reads "Effective Date: October 21st, 2026" (the previous version was dated September 3, 2026). Open Terms Archive recorded the new version on October 21, and the only substantive edit is a new Section 17, "Customer Support": "SHEIN may, in its discretion, assist with questions about the shipment, delivery, return, refund, or payment of an order, and will handle customer service inquiries in a reasonable manner. Unless a SHEIN policy expressly states otherwise, any such assistance is a courtesy and does not create a contractual obligation for SHEIN to resolve issues caused by carriers, banks, payment processors, Third-Party Sellers, or other third parties. Where a third party's involvement is necessary to resolve your issue, we may refer you to that third party." The old Section 17, "International Users," becomes Section 18. Nothing else in the U.S. terms changed. What it means: when a problem traces back to someone other than Shein, such as a delivery company losing or delaying a package, a bank or payment processor declining or double-charging a payment, or one of the marketplace sellers whose items Shein lists, the terms now say Shein's help is voluntary and that it can send you to that company instead. This matters because Shein is also a marketplace. Its terms already say the Services "enable users in the United States to purchase products from third-party sellers" and that "SHEIN neither owns nor takes title to any product sold by a Third-Party Seller" and "is not an auctioneer, seller, or carrier of any items that are listed for sale by Third-Party Sellers." The new wording has a limit: "Unless a SHEIN policy expressly states otherwise." Shein's existing written promises still apply. For example, the same terms still say claims for "loss or damage during delivery of the order by the carrier" must be made to Customer Service "within fourteen (14) days" of when you received the goods or should have received them, and that if Shein finds the claim valid it will replace the item or reimburse the purchase price and shipping. Returns still follow Shein's Return Policy. So the change mainly covers everything those policies don't spell out, and it gives Shein written backing for telling you to contact the carrier, your bank or the seller. Like all changes to Shein's terms, it applies as soon as it's posted: Section 1.2 says "By continuing to use or access any of the Services ... after the posting of changes, you accept and agree to any such changes." The arbitration agreement and its 30-day opt-out (written notice to us_legal@sheingroup.com within 30 days "after first becoming subject to this Arbitration Agreement") are unchanged. Coverage (checked October 21): we found no news reports or social posts about the new section. What you can do: for a lost or damaged package, file with Shein Customer Service within 14 days, since that promise is written into the terms. Save order pages, chat transcripts and tracking screenshots. If Shein or a seller won't fix a charge for something you never got or that wasn't delivered as agreed, you can dispute it with your card issuer. For credit cards, the FTC says the Fair Credit Billing Act dispute letter must reach the issuer "within 60 days after the first bill with the error was sent to you."

Who it affects

Anyone who shops on Shein's U.S. website or app, especially buyers of items sold by Third-Party Sellers on Shein's marketplace and anyone dealing with a lost package or a payment problem
