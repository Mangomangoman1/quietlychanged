Stripe privacy policy took effect Nov 20, 2026: AI voice agents, card-issuer data sharing, agentic payments

The Stripe Privacy Policy we previewed on Nov 3 is now the live version, dated Nov 20. It covers AI voice-agent calls, data sent to your card's bank and payments made by AI agents.

Took effect Nov 202 days ago

The wording

struck removed underlined added

What changed

The rewritten Stripe Privacy Policy we covered on November 3 is now the live version. Our November 20 snapshot of stripe.com/privacy still opened with "This Privacy Policy will be updated on November 20, 2026. Please review the upcoming changes here"; by November 21 that notice was gone and the page reads "Last updated: November 20, 2026" above the new text.

The live policy carries the changes we reported from the preview. It says Stripe "may leverage automated dialing systems and AI technologies, such as AI-powered voice agents and AI transcription, chat, and email tools, to initiate and conduct communications with you for operational, support, sales, marketing, and lead qualification purposes." During payment authorization Stripe "may also share fraud risk signals—including Stripe's risk assessment, and your IP address, email address, phone number, and shipping address—with your card-issuing bank." Stripe's fraud tools "may collect device and behavioral signals at the time of account registration and login on platforms that have enabled Stripe's risk API services." The Global Privacy Control section now applies "for the device and associated browser that transmits the signal."

Other changes: the old Link section is replaced by a Financial Connections section and a new "Agentic Payments" section: "Stripe offers services that allow you to authorize an AI agent, AI platform, or other automated software program (“agent operator”) to initiate purchases and payments on your behalf." Stripe "may share your payment credential and/or the associated token, and relevant transaction data, with the agent operator, merchant, payment networks, financial institutions" and others, and "For Link connected agents, you can view, modify, and revoke the scope of agent authorizations at any time through your Link account settings." Link itself is now covered by a separate Link Privacy Policy. For business contacts, a new "Business Lead and Prospect Information" paragraph says Stripe "may collect information about you and your business from third-party enrichment services and publicly available sources," including "your organization’s current payment service and billing providers." A new "Stripe profile" lets businesses be "discoverable to other merchants on Stripe."

What you can do: say so at the start of a Stripe call if you don't want it recorded, review agent authorizations and data-sharing settings in your Link account, and turn on Global Privacy Control in your browser (re-send it after clearing cookies).

Who it affects

Anyone who has paid through a Stripe-powered checkout or has a Link account, plus businesses and sales leads Stripe contacts
