Robinhood Class Action Service: auto-enrollment on Nov 18, 2026 and a 20% fee (how to opt out)

Robinhood will file class-action claims for you by default and keep 20% of the payout. In those cases you also lose the right to sue. You can unenroll.

Auto-enrollment starts Nov 18in 21 days

The wording

What changed

Robinhood's Customer Agreement (revised October 1, 2026) adds Section 36A, a "Class Action Service" whose terms take effect "on or after November 18, 2026." It reads: "Your Account is automatically enrolled in Robinhood's Class Action Service (the "Class Action Service"), unless you unenroll as described in this Section 36A." Robinhood and a third-party vendor will automatically file claims for you in securities class-action settlements involving stocks held in your account, and the RHF Fee Schedule sets the Class Action Service Fee at "20% of the class action settlement proceeds received on your behalf."

Enrollment lets Robinhood appoint the vendor "as your agent and attorney-in-fact," binds you to the settlement's releases, and means "you cannot separately bring a lawsuit, at any time, against any named defendant" in a case where a claim was filed for you. You also can't submit your own claim form for that settlement. Robinhood says it will notify you before each filing. You get seven business days to exclude that case, and you can unenroll from the service at any time through the app or Robinhood Customer Service.

The September 1, 2026 version of the agreement had no such section. Settlement administrators don't usually charge anything to file your own claim form, so staying enrolled by default costs you a fifth of any payout. Other brokers run similar auto-enrolled services, but the cut varies: E*TRADE from Morgan Stanley's vendor deducts 6% of payments (etrade.com class action terms), Edward Jones' administrator Broadridge deducts 15% (Edward Jones service disclosure), and Interactive Brokers charges a 20% contingency fee (IBKR Securities Class Action Recovery page), so Robinhood's 20% sits at the top of that range.

Checked October 16, 2026.

Who it affects

Robinhood brokerage customers, especially anyone holding individual stocks that later become part of securities class-action settlements

Quick answers

What is Robinhood's Class Action Service?

A new section of Robinhood's Customer Agreement (Section 36A, revised October 1, 2026) under which Robinhood and a third-party vendor automatically file claims for you in securities class-action settlements involving stocks you held. Its terms are effective on or after November 18, 2026.

How much does the Robinhood Class Action Service cost?

Robinhood's fee schedule sets the Class Action Service Fee at 20% of the settlement proceeds received on your behalf. Filing your own claim form with a settlement administrator usually costs nothing.

Am I automatically enrolled?

Yes. The agreement says: "Your Account is automatically enrolled in Robinhood's Class Action Service ... unless you unenroll." Closed accounts are not included.

How do I opt out of Robinhood's Class Action Service?

You can cancel your enrollment at any time in the Robinhood app or by contacting Robinhood Customer Service. To skip a single case while staying enrolled, decline within seven business days of Robinhood's notice about that lawsuit; otherwise the claim is filed for you. Unenrolling after a claim is filed doesn't pull you out of that case.

Do I give up any rights if Robinhood files a claim for me?

Yes. Once a claim is filed for you, you "cannot separately bring a lawsuit, at any time, against any named defendant" in that case, and you're bound by the settlement's releases. If you unenroll, you can still submit a claim form directly to the claims administrator yourself.
