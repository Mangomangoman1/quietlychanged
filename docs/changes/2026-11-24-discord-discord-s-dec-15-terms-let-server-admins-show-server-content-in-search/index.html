Discord terms update Dec 15, 2026: server content in search results, new arbitration rules

Discord's updated terms, in force December 15, say admins can have server content "appear off of Discord (such as in search engine results)", and hand all arbitrability questions to the arbitrator.

Takes effect Dec 15in 20 days

The wording

struck removed underlined added

What changed

Discord has posted new [Terms of Service](https://discord.com/terms) ("Last Updated: November 23, 2026", "Effective: December 15, 2026"), and they are already the version on the page.

Change 1: Server content outside Discord. The old terms said server owners and admins control "whether to make their server available in Server Discovery" and whether to publish invite links. The new terms say "Server admins decide what features their servers have and control the visibility of their servers, including the content within subject to certain user controls", and that admins can "have certain content from the server appear off of Discord (such as in search engine results)". The content section adds that server owners may "enable certain server content to appear off of Discord. Anyone can access this content." The terms do not say which servers are eligible or what the "user controls" are.

Change 2: Deleting content and accounts. Discord may now remove content "for any reason, including if we determine it is in breach of these terms", and adds "we make no promises or guarantees to you or others that we will or will not block, remove and/or permanently delete content from the services, or suspend or terminate any accounts." Account termination moves from "for any reason, or for the following reasons" to "for any reason, including but not limited to the following reasons".

Change 3: Arbitration. Before you can sue or arbitrate, the informal-dispute step now "requires submitting Discord's Dispute Resolution Form available at dis.gd/disputes completely and accurately" (it used to be an email to disputes@discordapp.com). A new "Delegation of Arbitrability" paragraph says "the arbitrator, and not any federal, state, or local court or agency, shall have exclusive authority to resolve any question, dispute, or controversy concerning the existence, scope, validity, enforceability, or applicability of this Agreement to Arbitrate". And people outside the U.S. and Canada who "CHOOSE TO INITIATE ARBITRATION" now also agree "TO THE APPOINTMENT OF A US ARBITRATOR". Each side also gets "the right to file early dispositive motions before the opening discovery in the arbitration."

Other changes: Account recovery now depends on "password recovery via the email or phone number associated with your account or multi-factor authentication methods"; a new sentence says Discord "may use automated systems to rank or show you things like notifications or sponsored content"; sponsored content is now described as "sponsored content on behalf of third parties"; the warranty disclaimer adds "WE CANNOT AND DO NOT REPRESENT OR WARRANT THAT THE SERVICES WILL ALWAYS BE SECURE OR ERROR-FREE"; and the line about Discord's list of minimum ages around the world was removed.

What stays the same: The minimum age is still 13, the class-action waiver still applies to U.S. and Canadian users, and the terms still say Discord "will provide 30 days’ notice of the date of any material changes to this Agreement to Arbitrate." Discord's privacy policy changed the same day; see our separate article.

Who it affects

Every Discord user, especially people who post in large or public servers, and U.S. and Canadian users bound by Discord's arbitration agreement

Quick answers

Will my Discord messages show up on Google?

Discord's new terms say server admins can "have certain content from the server appear off of Discord (such as in search engine results)". They don't say which servers qualify. Direct messages aren't mentioned; content in public or Discovery servers is the content the terms describe as accessible to anyone.

When do Discord's new terms take effect?

December 15, 2026, per the "Effective" date at the top of discord.com/terms.

How do I start a dispute with Discord now?

By "submitting Discord's Dispute Resolution Form available at dis.gd/disputes completely and accurately"; the email address disputes@discordapp.com was removed from the terms.
