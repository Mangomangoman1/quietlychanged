Grammarly / Superhuman privacy policy update Nov 2026: GPTZero, agent prompts, licensed social profiles

Superhuman's privacy policy now covers GPTZero and your prompts to its AI agents, and says it may license social media profile info about users and non-users from third parties.

The wording

struck removed underlined added

What changed

Superhuman Platform Inc. (formerly Grammarly) published a revised privacy policy covering Grammarly, Docs (formerly Coda), Superhuman Go, Superhuman Mail and now GPTZero.

Change 1: GPTZero joins. GPTZero, LLC is added to the list of subsidiaries, and "GPTZero" to the products the policy covers.

Change 2: licensed profile data. The old policy said Superhuman may collect publicly available information and "license data such as your contact info or information about your company or job function from third parties." The new one says it "may collect or license information about users and non-users from third parties, such as contact information, information about company or job function, or links to social media and online profiles." The California disclosure table now lists professional information as including "social media profile information, which we may license from others", and account information now includes "general location".

Change 3: other users can see some of it. A new disclosure category, "Other users of our products", says: "For certain products, some of this information may be visible to other users of that product." It's also added to the list of recipients in the state-law section.

Change 4: AI agents. User content now includes "prompts, or instructions to our agents", and a new example says "if you use our agent products, we process your prompts and usage data to provide you personalized responses and take the actions you request."

Other changes: a new line says that if you're under the age of consent and use the products through an Organization, "your Organization is responsible for providing consent"; the opt-out instructions now say to use the account-settings control or email privacy@superhuman.com with "Opt Out" in the subject line; the European legitimate-interest basis now covers providing the service "to you and/or to our other users". What stays the same: "we restrict our AI service providers from training their models on user content of Superhuman customers."

Who it affects

Grammarly, Superhuman Mail, Superhuman Go, Docs (Coda) and GPTZero users, and people who aren't users but whose details could be licensed from data providers
