Robinhood KPI binary options fees from Jan 1, 2027: the formula explained

Robinhood's earnings-metric contracts are free only through Dec 31, 2026. From Jan 1 a fee formula kicks in: 3 cents on a 50-cent contract without Gold, 1.75 cents with it.

Fees start Jan 1, 2027in 49 days

struck removed underlined added

What changed

Robinhood Financial's fee schedule, revised Nov 10, 2026 (the PDF's own timestamp), adds a new row for KPI Binary Options, the yes/no contracts Cboe lists on company metrics such as earnings per share, revenue or a company's subscriber count. Each contract pays $1 if the reported figure meets the strike and nothing if it doesn't, so its price p sits between $0 and $1.

Change 1: The fees are free only for now. Both the exchange fee and Robinhood's contract fee read "$0 through December 31, 2026", followed by "Effective January 1, 2027" and a formula.

Change 2: The formula. Robinhood's Contract Fee is k × p × (1 − p) × c, with "k = 10% without a Robinhood Gold subscription" and "k = 5% with a Robinhood Gold subscription". The Exchange Fee uses the same formula with "k = 2%" ("k = 5% for 'Professional' orders"). Both are "rounded up to the nearest $0.01 increment". The contracts are also subject to the Regulatory Fee, Trading Activity Fee and CAT fee, with the CAT fee "reduced to 1/100 of the standard rate".

What that means in dollars

Our arithmetic from the published formula, before the small regulatory fees: 100 contracts at 50 cents cost $50; from Jan 1 that order would carry a $2.50 contract fee plus a $0.50 exchange fee, $3.00 in total, or 6% of the stake ($1.75, or 3.5%, with Gold). Because p × (1 − p) shrinks more slowly than the price, cheap long shots pay more as a share of the stake: 100 contracts at 10 cents ($10) would carry $0.90 + $0.18 = $1.08, about 10.8%. At 90 cents the same $1.08 is about 1.2% of the $90 stake. The most any single contract can cost without Gold is 3 cents, at a 50-cent price.

What stays the same: Stock and ETF trades stay commission-free, and the footnote says the $0.50-per-contract "Professional" options fee "does not apply to KPI Binary Options"; professional orders pay the 5% exchange-fee rate instead.

The rest of today's fee-schedule diff was the PDF's layout being reflowed; the transfer, wire, margin and debit card fees read the same as before.

Who it affects

Robinhood customers who trade KPI binary options, the Cboe-listed yes/no contracts that pay $1 if a company's reported metric (like EPS or revenue) hits a strike; cheap long-shot contracts are hit hardest as a share of what you pay

Quick answers

Are Robinhood KPI binary options free?

Through December 31, 2026, yes: the fee schedule lists the exchange fee and contract fee as $0 until then. From January 1, 2027 both follow the formula k × p × (1 − p) × contracts.

How much will a KPI binary option cost to trade in 2027?

Without Gold, up to 3 cents per contract (at a 50-cent price) plus small regulatory fees; with Gold, up to 1.75 cents. On 100 contracts at 50 cents that's $3.00 without Gold or $1.75 with it.

Does Robinhood Gold lower the fee?

Yes. Robinhood's contract fee uses k = 5% with Gold instead of 10%; the 2% exchange fee is the same.

What you can do

  • If you trade KPI binary options, note that the fee-free period ends Dec 31, 2026
  • Before placing an order in 2027, work out the fee: multiply 12% (7% with Gold) by p × (1 − p) by the number of contracts, where p is the price in dollars
  • Check the current fee schedule at cdn.robinhood.com (RHF Fee Schedule PDF) before trading, since Robinhood says regulatory fees can change without notice
